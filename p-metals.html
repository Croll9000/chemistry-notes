Where They Are

The staircase line separates the metals from the nonmetals.

Metals and Nonmetals

Copper is used in electrical wiring, aluminum is rolled into foil and sulfur is used to make rubber tires stronger. Elements are chosen for these uses based on their properties, and these properties are closely tied to where an element sits on the Periodic Table.

  • Elements are classified as metals, nonmetals or metalloids based on their properties.
  • We can identify whether an element is a metal, nonmetal or metalloid from its position on the periodic table.
    • The periodic table has a dividing line that looks like a “staircase.”
  • Elements to the left of the staircase (except hydrogen) are metals.
    • Most elements are metals.
  • Elements to the right of the staircase are nonmetals.
    • Hydrogen is a nonmetal, even though it sits on the left side.
  • Elements that touch the staircase (except aluminum) are metalloids.
    • B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po and At

Properties

Metals, nonmetals and metalloids have different physical and chemical properties.

Properties of Metals

  • Found left of the staircase
  • Good conductors of heat and electricity
  • Lustrous – shiny
  • Malleable – able to be hammered into different shapes
  • Ductile – able to be drawn into wires
  • Lose electrons and form positive ions
  • Mostly solids at STP, with high melting points
    • Mercury is the only metal that is a liquid at STP.

Properties of Nonmetals

  • Found right of the staircase
  • Poor conductors of heat and electricity
  • Brittle – shatter or crumble when hit (as solids)
  • Dull – not shiny
  • Gain electrons and form negative ions
  • Many are gases at STP, with low melting and boiling points
    • Bromine is the only nonmetal that is a liquid at STP.

Metalloids

Silicon is the main material in nearly every computer chip and solar panel. It is useful because it conducts electricity better than a nonmetal but not as well as a metal, a property shared by the small group of elements known as metalloids.

  • Metalloids – elements that touch the staircase on the periodic table (except aluminum).
    • They share some properties of both metals and nonmetals.
  • Metalloids are shiny like metals.
  • Metalloids are brittle like nonmetals.
  • Semiconductors – materials whose conductivity can be good or poor depending on what they are combined with.
    • Ex: silicon is the semiconductor in computer chips and solar panels.

Metallic Character

Metallic character changes across a period and down a group.

Metallic Character

  • As you move from left to right across the periodic table, the metallic character of elements decreases.
    • The nonmetallic character of elements increases.
  • The most metallic elements are found on the left.
  • The most nonmetallic elements are found on the right.
  • Moving down a group, metallic character increases.
    • Ex: group 15 goes from nitrogen (nonmetal) to arsenic and antimony (metalloids) to bismuth (metal).
    • The most metallic elements are at the bottom left (cesium and francium).

Phases of the Elements

Solid, liquid or gas at standard temperature and pressure.

Phases at STP

Most elements are solids at room temperature, but a few are gases and only two are liquids. Since the temperature and pressure of a room change from day to day, chemists compare phases at an agreed-upon set of standard conditions.

  • STP – standard temperature and pressure: 273 K (0 °C) and 1 atm (101.3 kPa).
  • Most elements are solids at STP.
  • Only two elements are liquids at STP: bromine (Br) and mercury (Hg).
  • Eleven elements are gases at STP.
    • Hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, fluorine and chlorine.
      • These five are diatomic gases: H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2.
    • The six noble gases: helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon and radon.
      • These are monatomic: they exist as single atoms.
  • All of the gases are nonmetals.
    • They are found at the upper right of the table (plus hydrogen).
