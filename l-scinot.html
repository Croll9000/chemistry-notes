Writing Numbers in Scientific Notation

Scientific Notation

Chemistry deals with some of the largest and smallest numbers in all of science. The diameter of a hydrogen atom is about 0.0000000001 m, while just 12 grams of carbon contains about 602,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 atoms. Numbers like these are difficult to write, read and compare in standard form.

  • Scientific Notation – a shorthand way of writing very large or very small numbers as a number multiplied by a power of 10.
  • A number in scientific notation has only a single nonzero digit in the ones place of the coefficient. All other digits follow the decimal point: #.## × 10y
  • The sign of the exponent tells you the size of the number.
    • A positive (+) exponent means a large number (10 or more).
    • A negative (−) exponent means a small number (less than 1).

Converting to Scientific Notation

  1. Rewrite the number, leaving off leading and trailing zeros (but not zeros in the middle of the number).
  2. Put the decimal point between the 1st and 2nd nonzero digits (#.###).
  3. Add “× 10y”, where y is the number of places the decimal point had to move.
    • Make y positive if the original number is larger than 1.
    • Make y negative if the original number is smaller than 1.
Ex 1: Convert 4,500,000 into scientific notation.
4,500,000 = 4.5 × 106The decimal moved 6 places to the left. The number is large, so the exponent is +6.
Ex 2: Convert 0.0000462 into scientific notation.
0.0000462 = 4.62 × 10−5The decimal moved 5 places to the right. The number is small, so the exponent is −5.

Converting from Scientific Notation

  1. Rewrite the number, leaving off the “× 10y”.
  2. Move the decimal point a number of places equal to the exponent, y.
    • Fill any empty places with zeros.
  3. The sign of y tells you which way to move.
    • If y is positive, move the decimal to the right (the number gets bigger).
    • If y is negative, move the decimal to the left (the number gets smaller).
Ex 3: Convert 7.98 × 106 into standard form.
7.98 × 106 = 7,980,000
Ex 4: Convert 7.2 × 10−7 into standard form.
7.2 × 10−7 = 0.00000072

Move the decimal point. The exponent changes so the value stays the same.

Calculators and Practice

Enter scientific notation the right way, then practice converting.

Scientific Notation on Your Calculator

  • Most calculators have a key for “× 10 to the power of”.
    • It may be labeled EE, EXP or ×10x.
    • On the calculator in the top bar of these notes, it is the ×10ⁿ key.
  • To enter 6.02 × 1023, press: 6.02, then the EE key, then 23.
    • Do not type “× 10” before pressing EE.
    • That would enter 6.02 × 1024, which is 10 times too big.
  • When entering numbers in scientific notation into your calculator, make sure to surround them with parentheses ( ), especially when dividing: 3.0 ÷ (1.5 × 103).

The display may show 6.02E23. That “E23” means “× 1023”.
