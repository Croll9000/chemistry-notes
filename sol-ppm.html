Parts per Million

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency limits fluoride in public drinking water to 4.0 mg per liter. A liter of water has a mass of about 1,000,000 mg, so this limit means 4.0 parts of fluoride for every million parts of water. For solutions this dilute, percents and molarities give awkwardly small numbers, so chemists use parts per million instead. In this section we will discuss parts per million and how to calculate it.

Parts per Million (ppm)

Parts per million compares the mass of a solute with the mass of the whole solution, scaled up so that very small amounts become easy-to-read numbers.

  • Parts per Million (ppm) – the number of grams of solute in every 1,000,000 grams of solution.
  • Parts per million is used for very dilute solutions, such as pollutants or minerals dissolved in water.
    • For water, 1 ppm is about 1 mg of solute in every liter.
  • The mass of the solution is the mass of the solute plus the mass of the solvent.
