Equivalences and Conversion Factors

A traveler exchanging money at an airport sees a sign that says 1 euro = 1.08 dollars. That single statement is all that is needed to convert in either direction, dollars to euros or euros to dollars. Units of measurement work the same way. Any statement that two amounts are equal can be turned into a tool for switching from one unit to the other.

Such a statement is called an equivalence, such as 1 foot = 12 inches. From it, we can write a conversion factor, a fraction whose numerator and denominator are the two equal quantities, such as 12 inches1 foot. Every equivalence gives two conversion factors, with either unit on top.

Equivalence – two equal quantities with different units.

Conversion Factor – fraction/ratio of one scientific unit to another where the numerator and denominator are equal quantities with different units.

Since the numerator and the denominator of a conversion factor are equal, every conversion factor is equal to 1. Multiplying a quantity by 1 does not change the amount; it only changes the unit the amount is written in.

  • Equivalence – two equal quantities with different units.
  • Conversion Factor – fraction/ratio of one scientific unit to another where the numerator and denominator are equal quantities with different units.

Dimensional Analysis

Converting 2.5 hours into minutes is a calculation most people can do in their heads: 2.5 × 60 = 150 minutes. Written out with units, it becomes 2.5 h × 60 min1 h = 150 min, and the “h” on top cancels the “h” on the bottom, leaving minutes. This method of tracking units through a calculation is known as dimensional analysis, and it works for every conversion in chemistry, however many steps it takes.

Dimensional Analysis (also called the factor-label method) – method of unit conversion which involves multiplying by fractions known as conversion factors.

In dimensional analysis, the given unit is placed in the denominator, and the desired unit in the numerator, so that the unwanted unit cancels. If the units cancel to leave only the desired unit, the setup is correct, so the units themselves check the work.

  • Dimensional Analysis (also called the factor-label method) – method of unit conversion which involves multiplying by fractions known as conversion factors.

Steps to Dimensional Analysis

  1. Write down the given number and unit you wish to convert.
  2. Identify the desired unit.
  3. Identify relevant equivalences.
  4. Set up the conversion factor(s) you wish to multiply by.
    • Place the given unit in the denominator.
    • Place the desired unit in the numerator.
  5. Cancel out units that are in both the numerator and denominator.
  6. Multiply the given number by all numbers in the numerator.
    • Divide by all numbers in the denominator.
  7. Check to see that the final answer has the appropriate units.
When entering numbers in scientific notation into your calculator, make sure to surround them with parentheses, ( ).

Why does this work? A conversion factor is equal to 1 (the top and bottom are the same amount), and multiplying by 1 never changes the size of a quantity. It only changes the units it is written in.

Two-Step Conversions

Sometimes you will not know an equivalence directly between the units you are converting (like kilometers and centimeters). In that case, convert to a common unit first: the base unit. Your 1st conversion factor converts into the base unit, and your 2nd converts from the base unit into the desired unit.

Ex: How many centimeters are in 0.25 km?
