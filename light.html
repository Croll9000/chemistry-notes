Part1

Light Is a Wave of Energy

Light

The light that reaches your eyes from the sun left its surface about 8 minutes and 20 seconds ago, after crossing 150 million kilometers of empty space. A sound wave could never make that trip, because sound needs matter, such as air or water, to travel through. Light needs nothing at all. It is a ripple in electric and magnetic fields, and it carries energy across the vacuum of space to warm the Earth.

Light is a type of wave, and it is sometimes referred to as an electromagnetic wave, since light waves are ripples in electric and magnetic fields. Light waves carry energy. Light can also be described as a stream of tiny packets of energy. A photon is a single packet, or quantum, of light energy.

Light matters in chemistry because light of specific energies can interact with the electrons in an atom. When an atom absorbs a photon, one of its electrons gains energy, and when an electron loses energy, the atom can give off a photon. Studying the light that atoms absorb and release is how scientists first learned how electrons are arranged.

  • Light is a type of wave.
    • It is sometimes referred to as an Electromagnetic Wave.
    • Light waves are ripples in electric and magnetic fields.
    • Light Waves carry energy.
    • A photon is a single packet, or quantum, of light energy.
    • Light of specific energies can interact with the electrons in an atom.
Part2

Describing Light Waves

Wavelength

The radio waves from an FM station are about 3 meters long, roughly the height of a room, while the waves of green light are only about 500 nanometers long, so small that two hundred of them would fit across the width of a human hair. Both are forms of light. The most obvious difference between them is the length of each wave.

Wavelength is the length of a single wave cycle, measured from crest to crest or trough to trough. A crest is the peak of a wave, while a trough is its lowest point. Wavelength is measured in meters (m), or, for visible light, in nanometers (nm), where 1 nm is one-billionth of a meter.

The wavelength of visible light determines its color. Red light has the longest wavelengths we can see, about 700 nm, while violet light has the shortest, about 400 nm, and every other color falls in between.

All light waves can be described using 2 properties, wavelength and frequency

  • Wavelength – the length of a single wave cycle, measured from crest to crest or trough to trough.
    • Unit – meters (m), nanometers (nm)
    • Crest – peak of a wave
    • Trough – lowest point on a wave

Frequency

Tune a car radio to 101.1 FM, and the number on the dial is a frequency: the station’s radio waves pass your antenna 101.1 million times every second. Each station sends out waves at its own frequency, and the radio picks out one station from all the others by tuning to that frequency alone.

Frequency is the number of wave cycles that are completed in 1 second. Picture standing still while waves pass you: frequency is how many crests go by each second. It is measured in hertz (Hz), which means cycles per second and can also be written s−1.

All light travels at the same speed, so frequency and wavelength are linked. If every wave moves at the same speed, then waves that are shorter must pass by more often. Thus, as the wavelength of light decreases, its frequency increases: the two are inversely related.

  • Frequency – the number of wave cycles that are completed in 1 second.
    • Picture standing still while waves pass you: frequency is how many crests go by each second.
    • Units: hertz (Hz), cycles/second, s−1
Both waves move at the same speed. Watch the stopwatch: in the same 1 second, 2 crests of the top wave pass the marker, but 6 crests of the bottom wave pass it. The bottom wave has the higher frequency, and its wavelength is shorter. All light travels at the same speed (3.00 × 108 m/s), so a shorter wavelength always means a higher frequency.

Energy of Light Waves

You can sit under a bright desk lamp for hours without any harm, but an afternoon at the beach without sunscreen can leave you badly sunburned. The difference is not how bright the light is, but what kind of light it is. Sunlight contains ultraviolet light, whose waves have a shorter wavelength and higher frequency than visible light. Each ultraviolet photon carries enough energy to damage the molecules in skin cells, while each photon of visible light does not.

The frequency and wavelength of a light wave determine its energy. Waves with a short wavelength and high frequency have a high energy, while waves with a long wavelength and low frequency have a low energy. Thus, energy and frequency are directly related, while energy and wavelength are inversely related.

This is why higher-energy radiation, such as ultraviolet light, X-rays and gamma rays, can damage living cells and DNA, and why X-ray technicians stand behind a shield. Lower-energy radiation, such as radio waves, passes harmlessly through our bodies every day.

  • The frequency and wavelength of a light wave determine its energy
    • Waves with a short wavelength and high frequency have a high energy
    • Waves with a long wavelength and low frequency have a low energy

Higher-energy radiation (ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays) can damage living cells and DNA. Lower-energy radiation (visible light, infrared, microwaves) is mostly absorbed as heat.

Part3

The Electromagnetic Spectrum

Electromagnetic Spectrum

A microwave oven heats food with waves about 12 cm long, a car radio picks up waves several meters long, and a dentist’s X-ray machine uses waves only about as long as an atom is wide. All three are the same kind of wave as visible light, just with different wavelengths. Visible light, every color we can see, is only a narrow slice of a much larger family.

The electromagnetic spectrum arranges all of these waves in order, from long-wavelength, low-energy radio waves, through microwaves, infrared, visible light and ultraviolet, to short-wavelength, high-energy X-rays and gamma rays. Since all of them travel at the same speed, moving across the spectrum toward shorter wavelengths always means higher frequency and higher energy.

Engineers choose a type of light for each technology by its position on the spectrum. Radio waves are long enough to pass around buildings, microwaves are absorbed by the water in food, and X-rays have enough energy to pass through soft tissue but not through bone.

Light waves (photons) come in different types based on their wavelengths, frequencies and energies. These different types of photons are often organized into an Electromagnetic Spectrum.
