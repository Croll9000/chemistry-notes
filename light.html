Light Waves

Light is an electromagnetic wave described by its wavelength and frequency. Different types of light carry different amounts of energy.

Part1

Light Is a Wave of Energy

Light travels as an electromagnetic wave, in packets of energy called photons.

Light

Although light is a form of energy, it has the ability to interact with matter. Light is often produced in chemical reactions and can be used to trigger certain chemical changes. The light emitted by atoms and molecules can even be used to identify them. Therefore, it is important that we understand what light is.

  • Light is a type of wave.
    • It is sometimes referred to as an Electromagnetic Wave.
    • Light waves are ripples in electric and magnetic fields.
    • Light Waves carry energy.
    • A photon is a single packet, or quantum, of light energy.
    • Light of specific energies can interact with the electrons in an atom.
Part2

Describing Light Waves

Two measurements describe every light wave, and together they decide its energy.

Wavelength

Light can be described in the same way as the ripples that spread across a pond when a stone is dropped into it. Like any wave, each type of light can be described by measuring its size and how often it repeats.

All light waves can be described using 2 properties, wavelength and frequency

  • Wavelength – the length of a single wave cycle, measured from crest to crest or trough to trough.
    • Unit – meters (m), nanometers (nm)
    • Crest – peak of a wave
    • Trough – lowest point on a wave

Frequency

Every radio station is identified by a number, such as 101.1 FM. That number is the frequency at which the station broadcasts, in millions of cycles per second. Frequency is one of the most important ways of describing any wave, including light.

  • Frequency – the number of wave cycles that are completed in 1 second.
    • Picture standing still while waves pass you: frequency is how many crests go by each second.
    • Units: hertz (Hz), cycles/second, s−1
Both waves move at the same speed. Watch the stopwatch: in the same 1 second, 2 crests of the top wave pass the marker, but 6 crests of the bottom wave pass it. The bottom wave has the higher frequency, and its wavelength is shorter. All light travels at the same speed (3.00 × 108 m/s), so a shorter wavelength always means a higher frequency.

Energy of Light Waves

Sunburns are caused by ultraviolet light, not visible light, even though both come from the sun. This is because different types of light carry different amounts of energy.

  • The frequency and wavelength of a light wave determine its energy
    • Waves with a short wavelength and high frequency have a high energy
    • Waves with a long wavelength and low frequency have a low energy

Higher-energy radiation (ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays) can damage living cells and DNA. Lower-energy radiation (visible light, infrared, microwaves) is mostly absorbed as heat.

Part3

The Electromagnetic Spectrum

All types of light, from radio waves to gamma rays, arranged by wavelength, frequency and energy.

Electromagnetic Spectrum

Visible light is only a small part of a much larger family of light waves. Radio waves carry signals to phones and radios, microwaves heat food and X-rays allow doctors to see inside the body. All of these are forms of light that differ in their energy.

Light waves (photons) come in different types based on their wavelengths, frequencies and energies. These different types of photons are often organized into an Electromagnetic Spectrum.
