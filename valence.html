The Electrons That Matter

Only the outermost electrons take part in reactions. They explain why elements in a group behave alike.

Outermost (Valence) Electrons

Not all electrons participate in chemical reactions. The most important electrons for bonding are the ones found in the outermost shell. We sometimes refer to these as valence electrons.

  • Valence Electrons – electrons found in the outermost energy level of an atom.
    • They determine the chemical properties of the atom.
    • They determine how it will react with other atoms.
  • Valence Shell – the outermost electron shell of an atom.

Valence Electron Patterns

Elements in the same group on the periodic table possess the same number of valence (outermost) electrons. Therefore, they possess similar chemical properties.

Every element in a group has the same number of valence electrons.

Part2

Getting a Full Valence Shell

Most atoms are most stable with 8 valence electrons, like the noble gases.

Stable Electron Configurations

Helium is used to fill balloons and argon is used inside light bulbs because neither reacts with the materials around it. These noble gases are among the least reactive elements known, and the reason lies in the arrangement of their outermost electrons.

  • Most atoms are stable when their outermost (valence) shell contains a total of 8 electrons.
    • Elements in group 18 (the last column) of the periodic table have full valence shells.
      • They are very stable.
      • These elements are known as Noble Gases.
    • Atoms will form chemical bonds in order to achieve 8 valence electrons.
      • They do this by gaining, losing or sharing electrons.
      • This pattern is often called the octet rule.
    • Exceptions to this rule include Hydrogen and Helium which are stable with only 2 valence electrons.
Neon has a stable electron configuration due to it possessing 8 valence electrons.
Helium is stable with only 2 valence electrons.
Both Neon and Helium are found in group 18 and classified as noble gases.

Why Valence Electrons Matter

  • Atoms with 1, 2 or 3 valence electrons (metals) tend to lose them and form positive ions.
    • Fewer valence electrons to lose → the more easily the atom reacts.
    • Group 1 metals are the most reactive metals.
  • Atoms with 5, 6 or 7 valence electrons (nonmetals) tend to gain electrons (or share them) to reach 8.
    • Group 17 elements are the most reactive nonmetals.
  • Atoms with 4 valence electrons usually share electrons.
  • Noble gases already have a full valence shell, so they are unreactive: they almost never form compounds.
  • Elements in a group have the same number of valence electrons, so one element can take the place of another in a compound.
    • Ex: radium (group 2) can replace calcium (group 2) in bones and teeth.
