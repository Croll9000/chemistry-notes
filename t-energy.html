Energy: Kinetic & Potential

Every chemical and physical change comes with an energy change. Energy is stored in the positions of particles (potential energy) and in their motion (kinetic energy).

Part1

Energy

The ability to do work or cause change.

Energy

A burning match, a melting ice cube and a cold pack all involve energy. Chemical reactions are always accompanied by changes in the energy of atoms, molecules and compounds as bonds are broken and formed. Energy is either gained or lost by the reactants in a chemical reaction, so it is important to understand what energy means in chemistry.

  • Energy – the ability to do work or cause change.
    • Unit: the joule (J) or the kilojoule (kJ). 1 kJ = 1000 J.
    • Measuring device: a calorimeter (see the Calorimetry page).
  • Energy comes in many different forms, including light, heat, electrical, nuclear and chemical energy.
  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form into another. The total amount of energy in an isolated system stays the same (the law of conservation of energy).
  • Energy must be added to a system to begin a chemical reaction.
  • Two forms of energy explain almost everything in this unit:
    • Potential energy: energy stored in the positions of particles.
    • Kinetic energy: energy of the motion of particles.
Part2

Potential Energy

Stored energy, due to position.

Potential Energy

Lifting a book onto a high shelf takes energy. That energy does not disappear: it is stored in the book because of where it sits, and it comes back out as motion if the book falls. Particles store energy the same way.

  • Energy is required to move an object against forces like gravity or the electrostatic force. This energy is stored in the system as potential energy.
    • Potential Energy – stored energy of an object due to its position in a system.
  • Particles are attracted to one another. Separating them requires energy, which is stored as potential energy because of where the particles are relative to one another.
    • The farther apart particles are, the more potential energy they have.
    • The closer together particles are, the less potential energy they have.
  • When particles are allowed to come together, the potential energy they had is converted into another form of energy, usually kinetic energy.
  • We can think of particles as though they are connected by a spring. Pulling the particles apart takes energy, which is stored in the stretched spring. When the particles come together, the potential energy stored in the spring decreases.

Kinetic Energy

Energy of motion.

Kinetic Energy

A slow bowling ball knocks over a few pins; a fast one scatters them all. Movement takes energy, and the faster something moves, the more energy it carries.

  • To put a particle into motion, energy must be given to it. Once it is moving, this energy is called kinetic energy.
    • Kinetic Energy – energy possessed by an object due to its motion.
  • When the speed of particles increases, their kinetic energy increases.
  • When the speed of particles decreases, their kinetic energy decreases.
  • Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a sample (see the Temperature page).
