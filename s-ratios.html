Mole Ratios

Fertilizer plants make millions of tons of ammonia, NH3, every year by combining nitrogen and hydrogen. To avoid wasting either gas, engineers have to feed them into the reactor in exactly the right proportions. The balanced equation gives those proportions, just as a recipe tells a baker how many eggs to use for each cup of flour.

  • The coefficients in a balanced chemical equation represent the moles of substances taking part in the reaction. The ratio between the moles in the equation can be used as a conversion factor to determine the amount of a substance that will be produced or consumed in the reaction.
    • Mole Ratio – ratio between the moles of any two substances in a balanced chemical equation.
  • Ex: N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3
    • The mole ratio of N2 to H2 is 1 : 3. The mole ratio of N2 to NH3 is 1 : 2. The mole ratio of H2 to NH3 is 3 : 2.

Mole Ratio Conversions

A chemist who knows how much of one substance reacts can predict how much of every other substance is used or made. This is how manufacturers decide how much raw material to order and how much product to expect.

  • The mole ratio between any 2 substances in a reaction can be treated as a conversion factor. This conversion factor can then be used to predict the amount of a substance consumed or produced in the reaction.
