Polar Molecules

A thin stream of water bends toward a charged balloon, while a stream of a nonpolar liquid does not. Whether a molecule has a partially positive end and a partially negative end depends on both its bonds and its shape.

  • Molecules, in addition to bonds, can be classified as polar or nonpolar. When a molecule is polar, there will be an uneven distribution of charge on one side of the molecule compared to the other.
  • Polar Molecules – molecules with an uneven (asymmetrical) distribution of charge. They have a partially positive end and a partially negative end.
  • Nonpolar Molecules – molecules with a symmetrical distribution of charge. They do not have a partially positive end and a partially negative end.
Water (H2O) is an example of a polar molecule. Due to its asymmetrical shape and the large difference in electronegativity between oxygen (3.44) and hydrogen (2.20), a difference of 1.24, there is an uneven distribution of charge across the molecule. This leaves oxygen with a partially negative charge and hydrogen with a partially positive charge.

Identifying Polar Molecules

  • In order for a molecule to be considered polar, it must meet the following conditions:
    1. The molecule must have polar bonds (electronegativity difference between 0.4 and 1.7).
    2. The molecule must have an asymmetrical shape.
  • Symmetrical – possessing the same features on opposite sides of a molecule.
  • Asymmetrical – possessing different features on opposite sides of a molecule.
In ammonia (NH3), the electronegativity difference between nitrogen (3.04) and hydrogen (2.20) is 0.84. This means that it possesses polar bonds. NH3 also has an asymmetrical shape. Notice how the atoms above the dotted line do not match the atoms below.

Molecular Symmetry

  • Symmetry refers to the property of having matching features on opposite sides of a molecule.
  • In order for a molecule to be considered symmetrical, it must possess symmetry across each side of the molecule (top vs. bottom, left vs. right, etc.).

Polar Bonds vs. Polar Molecules

  • Just because a molecule has polar bonds does not necessarily mean that it is a polar molecule. In addition to having polar bonds, it must also possess an asymmetrical shape.
In a molecule of carbon dioxide (CO2), the electronegativity difference between carbon (2.55) and oxygen (3.44) is 0.89. This means that it possesses polar bonds. However, the electronegative oxygens are pulling electron density in opposite directions, which cancel one another out. The molecule has a symmetrical distribution of charge, so it does not have a partially positive end and a partially negative end. We classify it as nonpolar.
