Types of Reactions

There are millions of known chemical reactions, far too many to learn one at a time. Fortunately, most of them follow a small number of patterns, in the same way that the millions of sentences in English are built from a few basic sentence structures. Once we recognize a reaction as a particular type, we can usually predict its products without ever doing the experiment.

Chemical reactions can be classified into 5 types: synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement and combustion. Each type has its own pattern. In the patterns, A and B stand for elements or positive ions, and X and Y stand for negative ions or the elements they come from. The clue to each type is the number and kind of substances on each side of the arrow.

    1. Synthesis
    2. Decomposition

    4. Double Replacement
    5. Combustion
  • Each type has a pattern. In the patterns below, A and B stand for elements or positive ions, and X and Y stand for negative ions or the elements they come from.

Synthesis Reactions

When two or more simple substances join to form one more complex product, the reaction is a synthesis reaction.

Synthesis Reaction – chemical reaction in which two or more simple reactants combine into a more complex product.

We can identify a synthesis reaction since there are fewer products than reactants. Two or more reactants form a single product. The reactants are often elements, and the product is a compound, as when iron slowly combines with oxygen to form rust.

Building complex substances from simpler ones is one of the main jobs of the chemical industry. The ammonia in fertilizer is made by combining nitrogen and hydrogen:

N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3

  • Synthesis Reaction – chemical reaction in which two or more simple reactants combine into a more complex product.
  • How to identify a synthesis reaction:
    • There are fewer products than reactants: two or more reactants form one product.
    • The reactants are often elements, and the product is a compound.
In this synthesis reaction, 2 simple substances, iron (Fe) and sulfur (S), combine to form a more complex product, iron(II) sulfide (FeS).

Decomposition Reactions

Breaking compounds apart is just as important as building them. When one compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances, the reaction is a decomposition reaction.

Decomposition Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a single reactant is broken down (decomposed) into two or more simple products.

A decomposition reaction is the reverse of a synthesis reaction, so there are more products than reactants. The reactant is always a compound, and the products are elements or simpler compounds.

Since the bonds in the compound must be broken, energy in the form of heat or electricity often has to be added. Hydrogen peroxide is an exception that decomposes readily on its own. Poured on a cut, it foams as an enzyme in the blood speeds up its breakdown into water and oxygen gas:

2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2

  • Decomposition Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a single reactant is broken down (decomposed) into two or more simple products.
    • Often energy (heat or electricity) must be added to break down a compound.
  • How to identify a decomposition reaction:
    • There are more products than reactants: one reactant forms two or more products.
    • The reactant is always a compound. The products are elements or simpler compounds.
In this decomposition reaction, a complex reactant, calcium carbonate (CaCO3), is heated and breaks down into 2 simpler substances, calcium oxide (CaO) and carbon dioxide (CO2).

Single Replacement Reactions

When a coil of copper wire is placed in a clear solution of silver nitrate, crystals of silver slowly grow on the wire, and the solution turns blue as copper ions enter it. The copper, which is more reactive than silver, has pushed the silver out of its compound and taken its place. This is known as a single replacement reaction.

Single Replacement Reaction – a chemical reaction in which one element exchanges places with another element in a compound.

In a single replacement reaction, there are the same number of reactants and products, since an element and a compound react to form a new element and a new compound. Notice that there is a free element on both sides of the reaction. This is the easiest way to recognize the type.

Mining companies use this reaction to recover metals. In the 1500s, miners in Spain noticed that iron tools left in the copper-rich water draining from a mine became coated with copper. Some copper mines still recover copper this way, passing the mine water over scrap iron.

  • Single Replacement Reaction – a chemical reaction in which one element exchanges places with another element in a compound.
  • How to identify a single replacement reaction:
    • There are the same number of reactants and products: an element and a compound react to form a new element and a new compound.
    • There is a free element on both sides of the reaction.
In this single replacement reaction, a lone element, zinc (Zn), reacts with a compound, copper(II) sulfate (CuSO4). The zinc replaces the copper to form zinc sulfate (ZnSO4) and copper metal (Cu).

Double Replacement Reactions

When a clear solution of lead(II) nitrate is poured into a clear solution of potassium iodide, a bright yellow solid suddenly appears and swirls through the liquid. The lead ions and the iodide ions have found each other and formed lead(II) iodide, a solid that does not dissolve in water. The ions of the two compounds have traded partners. This is called a double replacement reaction.

Double Replacement Reaction – a chemical reaction in which an ion in one compound is exchanged with an ion in another compound.

In a double replacement reaction, positive ions replace positive ions, and negative ions replace negative ions. There are the same number of reactants and products, since two compounds react to form two new compounds, and there are no free elements on either side. The reactants are often dissolved in water (aq), and one product is often a solid, water or a gas.

  • Double Replacement Reaction – a chemical reaction in which an ion in one compound is exchanged with an ion in another compound.
    • In double replacement reactions, positive ions replace positive ions, and negative ions replace negative ions.
  • How to identify a double replacement reaction:
    • There are the same number of reactants and products: two compounds react to form two new compounds.
    • There are no free elements on either side of the reaction.
    • The reactants are often dissolved in water (aq), and one product is often a solid, water or a gas.
In this double replacement reaction, the sodium ion (Na+) exchanges places with the silver ion (Ag+) to form sodium nitrate (NaNO3) and silver iodide (AgI).

Combustion Reactions

Most of the energy used to heat homes, generate electricity and power cars comes from burning fuels such as natural gas, propane and gasoline. Each of these fuels is a hydrocarbon, a compound made of only hydrogen and carbon. When a hydrocarbon burns, it reacts with oxygen and releases large amounts of energy as heat and light. This is referred to as a combustion reaction.

Combustion Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a hydrocarbon (a molecule containing only hydrogen and carbon) reacts with oxygen (O2) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).

We can identify a combustion reaction since a hydrocarbon reacts with oxygen (O2), and the products are always carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O). Combustion reactions are exothermic, and that released energy is the whole reason we burn fuels. The carbon dioxide they produce is also the main gas linked to climate change.

  • Combustion Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a hydrocarbon (a molecule containing only hydrogen and carbon) reacts with oxygen (O2) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
    • Combustion reactions are exothermic, meaning they release heat.
  • How to identify a combustion reaction:
    • A hydrocarbon (containing carbon and hydrogen) reacts with oxygen (O2).
    • The products are always carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
Methane (CH4), a simple hydrocarbon, can burn in the presence of oxygen (O2). This produces carbon dioxide (CO2) gas and water (H2O) vapor. Heat is also released, which can trigger more combustion reactions as long as fuel is available.
To identify the type of reaction, count the substances (different formulas), not the number of molecules. CH4 + 2O2 has 2 reactant substances: methane and oxygen.
