Types of Reactions

  • Chemical reactions can be classified into 5 different types:
    1. Synthesis
    2. Decomposition
    3. Single Replacement
    4. Double Replacement
    5. Combustion
  • Each type has a pattern. In the patterns below, A and B stand for elements or positive ions, and X and Y stand for negative ions or the elements they come from.

In the animations, ionic compounds are drawn as groups of atoms so you can follow where each atom goes.

Synthesis Reactions

  • Synthesis Reaction – chemical reaction in which two or more simple reactants combine into a more complex product.
In this synthesis reaction, 2 simple substances, iron (Fe) and sulfur (S), combine to form a more complex product, iron(II) sulfide (FeS).

Decomposition Reactions

  • Decomposition Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a single reactant is broken down (decomposed) into two or more simple products.
    • Often energy (heat or electricity) must be added to break down a compound.
In this decomposition reaction, a complex reactant, calcium carbonate (CaCO3), is heated and breaks down into 2 simpler substances, calcium oxide (CaO) and carbon dioxide (CO2).

Single Replacement Reactions

  • Single Replacement Reaction – a chemical reaction in which one element exchanges places with another element in a compound.
In this single replacement reaction, a lone element, zinc (Zn), reacts with a compound, copper(II) sulfate (CuSO4). The zinc replaces the copper to form zinc sulfate (ZnSO4) and copper metal (Cu).

Double Replacement Reactions

  • Double Replacement Reaction – a chemical reaction in which an ion in one compound is exchanged with an ion in another compound.
    • In double replacement reactions, positive ions replace positive ions, and negative ions replace negative ions.
In this double replacement reaction, the sodium ion (Na+) exchanges places with the silver ion (Ag+) to form sodium nitrate (NaNO3) and silver iodide (AgI).

Combustion Reactions

  • Combustion Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a hydrocarbon (a molecule containing only hydrogen and carbon) reacts with oxygen (O2) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
    • Combustion reactions are exothermic, meaning they release heat.
Methane (CH4), a simple hydrocarbon, can burn in the presence of oxygen (O2). This produces carbon dioxide (CO2) gas and water (H2O) vapor. Heat is also released, which can trigger more combustion reactions as long as fuel is available.
To identify the type of reaction, count the substances (different formulas), not the number of molecules. CH4 + 2O2 has 2 reactant substances: methane and oxygen.
