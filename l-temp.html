What Temperature Measures

Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles.

Temperature

Weather reports in the United States give temperatures in degrees Fahrenheit, while most of the world and nearly all scientists use the Celsius scale. Chemists also rely on a third scale, the Kelvin scale, when working with gases and energy. Each scale measures the same thing starting from a different point.

  • Temperature – a measurement of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a sample.
    • Unit – degrees Celsius (°C) and kelvin (K)
    • Measuring device – thermometer
  • Kinetic Energy – the energy of motion.
    • The higher the temperature, the faster the particles move on average.
  • Temperature does not depend on how much of the substance you have.
    • Ex: a small cup and a large pot of boiling water are both at 100 °C.
    • Their particles have the same average kinetic energy.

The Celsius and Kelvin Scales

The Celsius and Kelvin scales have the same size degrees but different zero points.

Celsius Scale

  • The Celsius scale (°C) is based on water: water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C (at standard pressure, 1 atm).
  • The space between those two points is divided into 100 equal degrees.
  • Anything colder than freezing water has a negative Celsius temperature.
    • Ex: dry ice is about −78 °C.

Kelvin Scale

  • The Kelvin scale (K) is the SI temperature scale.
  • It starts at absolute zero (0 K), the lowest possible temperature.
    • At absolute zero, particles have the least possible kinetic energy.
    • Particles nearly stop moving.
  • How do we know where absolute zero is?
    • As a gas cools, its volume shrinks in a straight line.
    • Extending that line to zero volume lands at −273.15 °C.
    • No temperature can be colder.
  • There are no negative Kelvin temperatures.
  • Kelvin temperatures are written with no degree sign.
    • Ex: 298 K, not 298 °K

Comparing the Two Scales

  • A change of 1 kelvin is the same size as a change of 1 Celsius degree.
    • The scales just start at different places.
    • Converting only requires adding or subtracting 273.15.

Converting Between Kelvin and Celsius

  • From the Mathematical Relationships table of your Reference Table: K = °C + 273.15
  • To find °C, subtract 273.15 from the Kelvin temperature: °C = K − 273.15
  • This is addition or subtraction, so round to the same decimal place as the temperature you were given.
Ex 1: Convert 25 °C into kelvin.
K = °C + 273.15 = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 → 298 K
25 °C has no decimal places, so round to the ones place.
Ex 2: Convert 305 K into °C.
°C = K − 273.15 = 305 − 273.15 = 31.85 → 32 °C
305 K has no decimal places, so round to the ones place.
Check your answer: a Kelvin temperature is always 273.15 larger than the same temperature in Celsius. If you converted to kelvin and got a smaller number, you subtracted instead of adding.
