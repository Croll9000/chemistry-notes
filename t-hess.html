Hess’s Law

Hess’s Law

Carbon monoxide is a poisonous gas produced when fuels burn without enough oxygen, and chemists need to know how much heat is released when it forms: C(s) + ½O2(g) → CO(g). However, this ΔH cannot be measured directly. Whenever carbon burns, some of it always burns completely to carbon dioxide, so the calorimeter measures a mixture of two reactions. The solution comes from a property of enthalpy that makes it possible to calculate the heat of a reaction without ever running it.

Enthalpy is a state function: a quantity whose value depends only on the initial and final states of a system, not on the path taken between them. Consider a hike up a mountain. The change in elevation from the parking lot to the summit is the same whether you take the short, steep trail or the long, winding one. The distance you walk depends on the path, but the change in elevation does not. In the same way, the ΔH of a chemical reaction depends only on the enthalpy of the reactants at the start and of the products at the end. Whether the reaction happens in one step or in several, the total heat absorbed or released is the same.

This idea is summed up in Hess’s law: if a chemical reaction can be written as the sum of other reactions, the enthalpy of the overall reaction is the sum of the enthalpies of those reactions. For carbon monoxide, two reactions that can be measured are used: carbon burning completely to CO2 (ΔH = −393.5 kJ) and CO burning to CO2 (ΔH = −283.0 kJ). Reversing the second reaction and adding it to the first leaves exactly C(s) + ½O2(g) → CO(g), so ΔH = −393.5 kJ + 283.0 kJ = −110.5 kJ. Notice that the sign of the second ΔH was switched when its reaction was reversed (see Manipulating Reactions and Their Enthalpies).

The challenge in a Hess’s law problem is deciding how to change each given equation. The trick is to look for substances that appear in only one of the given equations, since each of those equations can only be placed one way to match the target. The steps to solving Hess’s law problems are:

  • Enthalpy is a state function, meaning that the only things that determine its value are its starting and ending states.
    • State Function – a quantity whose value depends only on the initial and final states of a system, not on the path taken between them.
    • For a chemical reaction, the steps by which reactants turn into products have no bearing on the heat released or absorbed by the process.
    • The only things that matter are the starting and ending enthalpies of the particles.
  • Hess’s Law – a chemical reaction can be expressed as the sum of other reactions, in which case the enthalpy of the overall reaction is the sum of the enthalpies of the component reactions.
  • Steps to solving Hess’s law problems:
    1. Identify the target equation.
    2. Find species unique to the target equation in the given equations.
    3. Manipulate the given equations by multiplying or dividing them by a factor, or by reversing them, in order to place the unique species in their appropriate spots based on the target equation. Apply all changes to the enthalpies as well.
    4. Sum the equations together and cancel out species present on both sides of the reaction.
    5. Sum the enthalpies together.
