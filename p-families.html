Families of the Periodic Table

Groups of elements with similar properties are called families. Each family has a location on the periodic table, properties to know for the Regents, and some interesting facts.

Main-Group Families

Groups 1, 2, 17 and 18.

Alkali Metals (Group 1)

Elements in the same group of the Periodic Table behave so similarly that they are often referred to as families. Several families have their own names, and the members of each share characteristics that make them useful in everyday life.

  • The elements in group 1 (except hydrogen) are known as the alkali metals.
  • Highly reactive
    • Never found uncombined (as a pure element) in nature
  • Most metallic elements
    • Very shiny
    • Very soft
  • Possess 1 valence electron
  • Form 1+ ions
  • Interesting facts
    • They are so reactive with air and water that they are stored under oil.
    • They are soft enough to cut with a knife.
    • They react with water to make hydrogen gas. The reaction gets more violent down the group: lithium fizzes, sodium skates around, potassium bursts into flame.
    • Lithium powers the rechargeable batteries in phones and laptops.

Alkaline Earth Metals (Group 2)

  • The elements in group 2 are known as the alkaline earth metals.
  • Very reactive, although not as reactive as group 1
  • Possess 2 valence electrons
  • Form 2+ ions
  • Interesting facts
    • Calcium compounds make up your bones and teeth, and seashells and chalk.
    • Magnesium burns with a blinding white light, so it is used in flares and fireworks.
    • Strontium compounds give fireworks their red color.

Halogens (Group 17)

  • The elements in group 17 are known as the halogens.
  • Highly reactive
  • Contain elements in all 3 phases of matter at room temperature
    • Gas: fluorine (F2) and chlorine (Cl2)
    • Liquid: bromine (Br2)
    • Solid: iodine (I2)
  • Diatomic: they exist as pairs of atoms (F2, Cl2, Br2, I2)
  • Possess 7 valence electrons
  • Form 1− ions
  • Interesting facts
    • “Halogen” means “salt former.” Halogens combine with metals to form salts (ionic compounds).
      • Ex: chlorine combines with sodium to form table salt, NaCl.
    • Fluorine is the most reactive nonmetal. Reactivity decreases down the group.
    • Chlorine is used to disinfect drinking water and swimming pools.
    • Fluoride in toothpaste helps prevent cavities.

Noble Gases (Group 18)

  • The elements in group 18 are known as the noble gases.
  • Inert – unreactive
  • Gases at room temperature
  • Possess 8 valence electrons (helium has 2)
    • Their valence shells are full.
  • Do not gain or lose electrons
  • Monatomic – found as single atoms under standard conditions
  • Interesting facts
    • Helium is less dense than air, so helium balloons float.
    • Neon glows red-orange in “neon” signs. Other noble gases glow other colors.
    • Argon makes up almost 1% of the air.
    • The larger noble gases (krypton and xenon) can react on rare occasions.

The Middle and the Bottom

Transition metals, lanthanides and actinides.

Transition Metals (Groups 3–12)

  • The elements in groups 3–12 are known as the transition metals.
    • They are the transition between the s-block (groups 1–2) and the p-block (groups 13–18).
  • Many form brightly colored solutions in water.
  • Some transition metals have multiple oxidation states (more than one possible ion charge).
    • Ex: iron forms Fe2+ and Fe3+; copper forms Cu+ and Cu2+.
  • Interesting facts
    • Familiar metals like iron, copper, silver, gold and nickel are transition metals.
    • Mercury is the only metal that is a liquid at room temperature.
    • Gold and platinum are so unreactive that they stay shiny for centuries, which is why they are used in jewelry.

Lanthanides and Actinides

Some of the most important materials in modern technology come from the two rows at the bottom of the Periodic Table. Neodymium is used to make the strong magnets in headphones and electric motors, and uranium is used as fuel in nuclear power plants.

  • Lanthanides – elements with atomic numbers 57–71
    • Metallic
    • Reactive
  • Actinides – elements with atomic numbers 89–103
    • Metallic
    • Reactive
    • Radioactive
  • They are placed below the table so it fits on a page. They really belong in periods 6 and 7.
  • Interesting facts
    • Neodymium (a lanthanide) makes the strongest permanent magnets, used in earbuds, electric cars and wind turbines.
    • Uranium and plutonium (actinides) are fuels for nuclear power.
    • Almost every element after uranium (92) exists only because scientists made it in a laboratory or nuclear reactor.

Hydrogen: A Family of Its Own

  • Hydrogen sits above group 1 because it has 1 valence electron.
  • Hydrogen is not an alkali metal.
    • It is a nonmetal and a diatomic gas (H2) at STP.
