Chemical Bonds

A chemical bond is the attraction that holds atoms together. Energy is required to break a bond.

Chemical Bond

Sometimes, when substances are mixed, something new can be created. For instance, if you’ve ever lit a match, baked a cake or used a battery, you’ve taken atoms of different elements and combined them into something new. You’ve formed what are known as chemical bonds.

  • Atoms can come together and form strong associations called chemical bonds.
    • Chemical Bond – strong attraction that holds atoms together
  • We can illustrate chemical bonds in different ways:
    1. Showing the 2 particles touching each other
    2. Drawing a line or dash between the particles

Bonds and Energy

  • Bonded atoms are more stable (have less energy) than the same atoms when they are separated.
  • To break a chemical bond, energy must be added (absorbed).
  • When a chemical bond forms, energy is released.

Add energy to a hydrogen molecule a little at a time until the bond breaks.

Part2

Molecules and Compounds

Bonded atoms make molecules. When the atoms are from different elements, the molecule is also a compound.

Molecules and Compounds

Most of the substances we encounter every day, such as water, table sugar and the carbon dioxide we exhale, are not made of single atoms. Instead, they are made of atoms bonded together in specific combinations.

  • When atoms combine, they form molecules
    • Molecule – two or more atoms that are chemically bonded (combined)
  • When atoms of different elements (types) form chemical bonds, the resulting particle is known as a compound.
    • The ratio of atoms of different elements in a compound is fixed.
    • Compound – two or more different elements chemically bonded (combined) in a fixed proportion
H2 would be classified as a molecule because it contains atoms that are chemically bonded. However, it is not a compound because it does not contain 2+ different elements.
H2O is both a molecule and a compound. It contains chemical bonds, making it a molecule. It also has 2+ different elements that are chemically combined.

Diatomic Elements

The air we breathe is roughly 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. Neither of these gases exists as individual atoms. Instead, their atoms are found bonded together in pairs.

  • Diatomic Elements – elements that are found as pairs of bonded atoms when they are uncombined (not part of a compound) at standard conditions.
    • Diatomic Elements – elements consisting of molecules made of two identical atoms bonded together under standard conditions
  • There are 7 diatomic elements: bromine (Br2), iodine (I2), nitrogen (N2), chlorine (Cl2), hydrogen (H2), oxygen (O2) and fluorine (F2).
  • Monatomic Elements – elements that are found as single atoms under standard conditions.
    • The prefix mono- means “one”.
    • The prefix di- means “two”.
Note how all of the diatomic elements consist of identical atoms bonded together. You can remember all 7 diatomics using the mnemonic “BrINClHOF”.

Chemical Formulas

A formula tells you which elements are in a compound and how many atoms of each.

Decoding Compounds

Every compound has a name, but names can be long and difficult to interpret. Chemists instead use a shorthand that shows exactly which elements are present and in what amounts, similar to how a recipe lists each ingredient and its quantity.

  • Every compound is described with a chemical formula
    • Chemical Formula – concise way to describe what elements and how many atoms of each are present in a compound
    • Subscripts – small numbers printed slightly below an element symbol.
      • They show how many atoms of that element are in a compound.
      • No subscript means 1 atom.

Fixed Proportions

Water (H2O) and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) are made of the same two elements, yet one is safe to drink and the other is used to disinfect cuts. The only difference between them is the number of oxygen atoms in each molecule. The ratio of atoms in a compound is fixed, and changing it creates a different substance.

Compounds are defined as having fixed proportions. This means that each compound has its own unique ratio among the elements that compose it. If you change the ratio of elements, you change the compound.

Water has the chemical formula H2O. This means that every molecule of water must have 2 atoms of hydrogen and 1 atom of oxygen.
Hydrogen peroxide is made of the same elements as water, hydrogen and oxygen. However, it has the chemical formula H2O2. This makes it an entirely different compound, with different chemical and physical properties.

Formulas with Parentheses

  • Some chemical formulas contain element symbols within parentheses.
    • These atoms form a group that stays together.
    • You’ll learn its name in a later unit.
  • Any subscript to the bottom right of the parentheses applies to all elements within the parentheses.
Mg(OH)2
Chemical formula: Mg(OH)2
Composition: 1 atom of magnesium (Mg), 2 atoms of oxygen (O), 2 atoms of hydrogen (H). The 2 outside the parentheses doubles both the O and the H.

Structure Decides Properties

The same atoms, bonded in a different arrangement, make a substance with different properties.

Allotropes

Diamond, the hardest natural material, and graphite, the soft gray material in pencils, are both made entirely of carbon atoms. Similarly, the oxygen we breathe (O2) and the ozone (O3) in the upper atmosphere are both made only of oxygen. The difference between these substances lies in how their atoms are bonded together.

  • Allotropes – different forms of the same element under standard conditions.
    • Their atoms are bonded together in different ways.
    • Allotropes – different physical and structural forms of the same element.
    • Substances with different structures possess different chemical properties and different physical properties.
Oxygen (O2) and ozone (O3) are allotropes of one another. They both consist of oxygen atoms, but an oxygen molecule has 2 atoms while an ozone molecule has 3 atoms. Oxygen is colorless while ozone has a pale blue color. Oxygen is odorless while ozone has a sharp odor. Oxygen is a stable molecule while ozone breaks down quickly and is much more reactive.

Allotropes of Carbon

Diamond and graphite are allotropes of each other. They are composed of atoms of the same element, carbon (C). However, these atoms are bonded differently in each substance. Diamond has a dense, 3-dimensional structure, while graphite consists of 2-dimensional sheets of hexagons layered on top of each other. Diamond is hard and clear while graphite is gray-black and soft/flaky. Graphite conducts electricity while diamond does not.
  • Structure → function: engineers choose materials because of how their particles are arranged.
    • Diamond’s atoms are locked in a 3-D network, so diamond-tipped saws can cut stone.
    • Graphite’s layers slide past each other easily, so graphite works as pencil “lead” and as a dry lubricant.
