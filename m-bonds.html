Chemical Bonds

Chemical Bond

Atoms rarely exist on their own. In most of the matter around you, atoms are held tightly to other atoms by strong attractions known as chemical bonds. Atoms that are bonded together move as a single particle.

Chemical Bond – strong attraction that holds atoms or ions together

For instance, hydrogen and oxygen are both colorless gases. If a balloon filled with a mixture of the two is touched with a flame, it explodes with a loud bang and leaves behind tiny droplets of water. During the explosion, the hydrogen and oxygen atoms let go of their original partners and bond to new ones. The water that forms does not fall apart on its own, and it takes a great deal of energy to separate it back into hydrogen and oxygen. This tells us that the atoms in a water molecule are held together very tightly.

Bonds are far too small to see, so chemists show them using models. One way is to draw the bonded atoms touching each other. Another is to draw a line between the atoms, with each line representing one bond. Both drawings mean the same thing: the atoms are held together and move as a single particle.

Chemical bonds are what allow about 90 naturally occurring elements to form millions of different substances. Which atoms are bonded, and how they are bonded, determines what the substance is. For example, a carbon atom bonded to two oxygen atoms forms carbon dioxide, CO2, the gas that you breathe out. A carbon atom bonded to only one oxygen atom forms carbon monoxide, CO, which is a poisonous gas.

  • Atoms can come together and form strong associations called chemical bonds.
    • Chemical Bond – strong attraction that holds atoms or ions together
  • We can illustrate chemical bonds in different ways:
    1. Showing the 2 particles touching each other
    2. Drawing a line or dash between the particles

Bonds and Energy

Recall that a chemical bond is a strong attraction that holds atoms together. Once atoms are bonded, they stay together on their own, and they are more stable, and have less energy, than the same atoms apart.

Think of two strong magnets that have snapped together. Pulling them apart takes real effort, and the stronger the magnets, the more effort it takes. Bonded atoms are the same. To separate them, energy has to be put in to pull them apart against the attraction holding them together. Thus, to break a chemical bond, energy must be added (absorbed).

Now picture the same magnets held a short distance apart and let go. They jump together with a click, and the energy comes out as motion and sound. When atoms form a bond, energy is given off in the same way, usually as heat. Thus, when a chemical bond forms, energy is released. Of course, atoms are not tiny magnets, since the attraction in a bond comes from electric charges, but the energy behaves the same way.

This idea explains why many substances do not react until they are given a push. A match sitting in a box does not burst into flames. The bonds in the match head and in the oxygen of the air must first be broken, and that takes energy. Striking the match supplies it. Once the reaction starts, the new bonds that form release more energy than was needed to break the old ones. The extra energy comes out as the heat and light of the flame, and that heat breaks the next bonds, keeping the match burning.

  • Bonded atoms are more stable (have less energy) than the same atoms when they are separated.
  • To break a chemical bond, energy must be added (absorbed).
  • When a chemical bond forms, energy is released.

Molecules and Compounds

Molecules and Compounds

When atoms bond together, the particle they form has a name. Any particle made of bonded atoms is referred to as a molecule. Oxygen gas (O2) is made of molecules, each one two oxygen atoms bonded together. Water (H2O) is also made of molecules, each one two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom.

Molecule – two or more atoms that are chemically bonded (combined)

These two molecules differ in one important way. An O2 molecule contains only one element. However, an H2O molecule contains two. When the bonded atoms belong to different elements, the substance is also called a compound.

Compound – two or more different elements chemically bonded (combined) in a fixed proportion

You can see that every compound is made of molecules or similar bonded particles, but not every molecule is a compound. O2 is a molecule but not a compound, since its atoms all belong to the same element. H2O is both a molecule and a compound.

The ratio of atoms in a compound is fixed. Every water molecule anywhere in the universe, from a raindrop to the ice on a comet, contains exactly two hydrogen atoms for every oxygen atom.

  • When atoms combine, they form molecules
    • Molecule – two or more atoms that are chemically bonded (combined)
  • When atoms of different elements (types) form chemical bonds, the resulting particle is known as a compound.
    • The ratio of atoms of different elements in a compound is fixed.
    • Compound – two or more different elements chemically bonded (combined) in a fixed proportion
H2 would be classified as a molecule because it contains atoms that are chemically bonded. However, it is not a compound because it does not contain 2+ different elements.
H2O is both a molecule and a compound. It contains chemical bonds, making it a molecule. It also has 2+ different elements that are chemically combined.

Diatomic Elements

The oxygen in the air you breathe is not made of single oxygen atoms. A lone oxygen atom is unstable, and it quickly bonds to the first oxygen atom it meets. As a result, each particle of oxygen gas is two oxygen atoms bonded together, O2. The nitrogen that makes up most of the air behaves the same way and exists as N2.

Elements like these are known as diatomic elements, since the prefix di- means “two.” There are 7 diatomic elements: bromine (Br2), iodine (I2), nitrogen (N2), chlorine (Cl2), hydrogen (H2), oxygen (O2) and fluorine (F2).

Diatomic Elements – elements found as molecules made of two identical atoms bonded together when they are uncombined (not part of a compound) at standard conditions.

Helium is different. Helium atoms do not bond to anything, not even to each other, so a helium balloon is filled with single, separate atoms. Elements like helium are called monatomic elements, from the prefix mono-, meaning “one.” Helium, neon and argon are monatomic, and so are the metals.

Monatomic Elements – elements that are found as single atoms under standard conditions.

Be careful when writing formulas. The diatomic rule only applies when an element is on its own. Once oxygen is part of a compound, such as H2O, the formula already shows exactly how many oxygen atoms are present, and no extra 2 is added.

  • Diatomic Elements – elements found as molecules made of two identical atoms bonded together when they are uncombined (not part of a compound) at standard conditions.
  • There are 7 diatomic elements: bromine (Br2), iodine (I2), nitrogen (N2), chlorine (Cl2), hydrogen (H2), oxygen (O2) and fluorine (F2).
  • Monatomic Elements – elements that are found as single atoms under standard conditions.
    • The prefix mono- means “one”.
    • The prefix di- means “two”.
Note how all of the diatomic elements consist of identical atoms bonded together. You can remember all 7 diatomics using the mnemonic “BrINClHOF”.

Chemical Formulas

Decoding Compounds

We already have symbols for the elements. To describe a compound, we also need to say how many atoms of each element it contains. This is the job of a chemical formula. The element symbols in a formula tell us what elements are present. Small numbers called subscripts tell us how many atoms of each there are.

Chemical Formula – concise way to describe what elements and how many atoms of each are present in a compound

Subscripts – small numbers placed to the bottom right of an element symbol. They indicate the number of atoms of that element in a molecule or compound.

A subscript belongs to the symbol right before it. In H2O, the 2 after the H shows two hydrogen atoms. The O has no subscript. No subscript means 1 atom, since writing a 1 would only add clutter. Thus, a water molecule contains three atoms in all.

Those small numbers matter a great deal. Carbon dioxide (CO2) is the harmless gas you breathe out. Carbon monoxide (CO) is produced when fuel burns without enough oxygen, as in a car engine or a gas furnace with a blocked vent, and it is a deadly poison. CO attaches to the hemoglobin in red blood cells about 200 times more strongly than oxygen does, so it blocks the blood from carrying oxygen to the body. The only difference between the two gases is one oxygen atom, and the formula shows it in a single subscript.

  • Every compound is described with a chemical formula
    • Chemical Formula – concise way to describe what elements and how many atoms of each are present in a compound
    • Subscripts – small numbers placed to the bottom right of an element symbol. They indicate the number of atoms of that element in a molecule or compound.
      • No subscript means 1 atom.

Fixed Proportions

Around 1800, chemists were still debating whether a compound could be made with a little more of one element and a little less of another, the way a cook might add more or less salt to a recipe. The French chemist Joseph Proust set out to answer this question. He carefully analyzed samples of the same compounds that came from many different places and had been made in different ways.

Proust found that each compound always contained its elements in exactly the same proportion by mass, no matter where the sample came from. From this he concluded that every compound has a fixed composition. For instance, water from a glacier, from the ocean or made in a laboratory always contains two hydrogen atoms for every oxygen atom.

If the ratio of the elements changes, the result is not a slightly different version of the same compound but a different substance altogether, with its own properties. Water, H2O, and hydrogen peroxide, H2O2, are both made of hydrogen and oxygen. You drink water every day, while hydrogen peroxide is used to clean cuts and to bleach hair.

Water has the chemical formula H2O. This means that every molecule of water must have 2 atoms of hydrogen and 1 atom of oxygen.
Hydrogen peroxide is made of the same elements as water, hydrogen and oxygen. However, it has the chemical formula H2O2. This makes it an entirely different compound, with different chemical and physical properties.

Formulas with Parentheses

Some chemical formulas contain parentheses. The atoms inside a set of parentheses are bonded together into a group that stays together as a unit. As a result, any subscript to the bottom right of the parentheses applies to all elements within the parentheses, much like multiplication in math.

For instance, milk of magnesia is an antacid that people take to settle an upset stomach. Its active ingredient, magnesium hydroxide, has the formula Mg(OH)2. The oxygen and hydrogen atoms inside the parentheses form an OH group, and the subscript 2 tells us that there are two of these groups for every magnesium atom.

Mg(OH)2 contains:

  • 1 magnesium atom
  • 2 × 1 = 2 oxygen atoms
  • 2 × 1 = 2 hydrogen atoms

If an atom inside the parentheses already has its own subscript, the two numbers are multiplied. Calcium nitrate, Ca(NO3)2, is used in fertilizers. It contains 1 calcium atom, 2 × 1 = 2 nitrogen atoms and 2 × 3 = 6 oxygen atoms.

  • Some chemical formulas contain element symbols within parentheses.
    • These atoms form a group that stays together.
  • Any subscript to the bottom right of the parentheses applies to all elements within the parentheses.
Mg(OH)2
Chemical formula: Mg(OH)2
Composition: 1 atom of magnesium (Mg), 2 atoms of oxygen (O), 2 atoms of hydrogen (H). The 2 outside the parentheses doubles both the O and the H.

Structure Decides Properties

Allotropes

Pure oxygen comes in two forms. The oxygen we breathe is a gas made of O2 molecules, but there is a second form, ozone, with the formula O3. Both are made of nothing but oxygen atoms, yet they behave very differently. Oxygen gas has no smell and keeps us alive. Ozone has a sharp smell, and when it forms near the ground in smog, it damages the lungs.

Oxygen and ozone are referred to as allotropes of oxygen. Their atoms are bonded together in different ways. An oxygen molecule has 2 atoms bonded together, and an ozone molecule has 3. It makes sense that a different structure produces a different substance, so allotropes have different chemical properties and different physical properties, even though they are made of the same element.

Allotropes – different physical and structural forms of the same element under standard conditions.

The third oxygen atom changes more than the smell. An ozone molecule absorbs ultraviolet light that passes straight through oxygen molecules. For this reason, the layer of ozone high in the atmosphere, about 15 to 35 km above the ground, shields the surface from most of the Sun’s harmful ultraviolet light, something the far more plentiful O2 cannot do.

  • Allotropes – different physical and structural forms of the same element under standard conditions.
    • Their atoms are bonded together in different ways.
    • Substances with different structures possess different chemical properties and different physical properties.
Oxygen (O2) and ozone (O3) are allotropes of one another. They both consist of oxygen atoms, but an oxygen molecule has 2 atoms while an ozone molecule has 3 atoms. Oxygen is colorless while ozone has a pale blue color. Oxygen is odorless while ozone has a sharp odor. Oxygen is a stable molecule while ozone breaks down quickly and is much more reactive.

Allotropes of Carbon

Carbon has more allotropes than almost any other element, and two of them have very different properties. Graphite is soft, gray and slippery. Diamond, on the other hand, is clear, sparkling and the hardest natural substance known. Yet both are pure carbon, made of exactly the same atoms.

The difference is entirely in how the atoms are arranged. In diamond, each carbon atom is bonded to four other carbon atoms in a three-dimensional network that repeats in all directions. There is no direction in which the crystal can easily split, so it is extremely hard. In graphite, the carbon atoms are bonded into flat sheets. The sheets are only weakly attracted to one another, so they slide apart easily. Every time you write with a pencil, layers of graphite slide off onto the paper.

Note that the “lead” in a pencil has never contained any lead. When a large deposit of soft, black graphite was found in England in the 1500s, people mistook it for a form of lead, and the name stuck.

This is a clear example of structure deciding function. Engineers choose materials because of how their particles are arranged. Since diamond’s atoms are locked in a three-dimensional network, diamond-tipped saws and drill bits are used to cut stone, concrete and even the rock beneath oil fields. Since graphite’s layers slide past one another, graphite is used as a dry lubricant in locks.

Diamond and graphite are allotropes of each other. They are composed of atoms of the same element, carbon (C). However, these atoms are bonded differently in each substance. Diamond has a dense, 3-dimensional structure, while graphite consists of 2-dimensional sheets of hexagons layered on top of each other. Diamond is hard and clear while graphite is gray-black and soft/flaky. Graphite conducts electricity while diamond does not.
  • Structure → function: engineers choose materials because of how their particles are arranged.
    • Diamond’s atoms are locked in a 3-D network, so diamond-tipped saws can cut stone.
    • Graphite’s layers slide past each other easily, so graphite works as pencil “lead” and as a dry lubricant.
