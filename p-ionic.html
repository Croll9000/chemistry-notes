How Ions Change Size

Ionic Radius

When an atom gains or loses electrons, its size changes along with its charge. The sizes of ions affect how closely they pack together in compounds such as table salt.

  • Recall that ions are atoms that have gained or lost electrons to acquire a net charge.
  • Ionic Radius – the radius of an atom’s ion after it has gained or lost electrons.
    • Unit – picometers (pm)
  • Metals – when an atom loses electrons, its ionic radius is smaller than its original atomic radius.
    • Fewer electrons in the atom means there is less electron-electron repulsion.
    • Main-group metals usually lose their whole outer shell, so the ion has one fewer shell than the atom.
  • Nonmetals – when an atom gains electrons, its ionic radius is larger than its original atomic radius.
    • With more electrons in the atom, there is stronger electron-electron repulsion.
    • The nuclear charge stays the same, so the bigger electron cloud spreads out.
Sodium, as a metal, loses an electron and forms a positive ion. Its ionic radius is smaller than its atomic radius.
Chlorine is a nonmetal and gains an electron when it forms an ion. Its ionic radius is larger than its atomic radius.
