Ionic Radius

When an atom gains or loses electrons, its radius changes. Metal ions are smaller than their atoms, and nonmetal ions are larger than their atoms.

How Ions Change Size

Ionic Radius

Lithium-ion batteries work because the small Li+ ion, with a radius of 76 pm, can slip between the layers of carbon atoms in the battery’s graphite electrode, while a lithium atom has a radius of 130 pm. Recall that ions are atoms that have gained or lost electrons. The ionic radius is the radius of an atom’s ion after it has gained or lost electrons, measured in picometers. When a metal atom loses electrons, its ionic radius is smaller than its atomic radius. Main-group metals usually lose their whole outer shell, so the ion has one fewer shell than the atom, and with fewer electrons there is also less repulsion between them. When a nonmetal atom gains electrons, its ionic radius is larger than its atomic radius. The nuclear charge stays the same, but the added electrons increase the repulsion, so the electron cloud spreads out.

  • Recall that ions are atoms that have gained or lost electrons to acquire a net charge.
  • Ionic Radius – the radius of an atom’s ion after it has gained or lost electrons.
    • Unit – picometers (pm)
  • Metals – when an atom loses electrons, its ionic radius is smaller than its original atomic radius.
    • Fewer electrons in the atom means there is less electron-electron repulsion.
    • Main-group metals usually lose their whole outer shell, so the ion has one fewer shell than the atom.
  • Nonmetals – when an atom gains electrons, its ionic radius is larger than its original atomic radius.
    • With more electrons in the atom, there is stronger electron-electron repulsion.
    • The nuclear charge stays the same, so the bigger electron cloud spreads out.
Sodium, as a metal, loses an electron and forms a positive ion. Its ionic radius is smaller than its atomic radius.
Chlorine is a nonmetal and gains an electron when it forms an ion. Its ionic radius is larger than its atomic radius.

Atoms and Their Ions

Every metal ion in the table is smaller than its atom, and every nonmetal ion is larger than its atom. Ions in the same group also follow the same trend as atoms, growing larger down the group: Li+ < Na+ < K+, and F− < Cl− < Br− < I−.
