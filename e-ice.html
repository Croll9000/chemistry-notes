ICE Tables

Chemists rarely measure every concentration in an equilibrium mixture. Usually they know the starting amounts and measure just one substance at equilibrium, perhaps by the color of the mixture. The balanced equation then lets them work out all of the others, since every substance changes in the ratio given by its coefficient. If 0.20 M of SO2 is used up in 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3, then 0.10 M of O2 must be used up and 0.20 M of SO3 must form.

An ICE table is a table that organizes the initial concentrations, the changes in concentration and the equilibrium concentrations of each substance in a reaction. The changes follow the coefficients of the balanced equation, so if x mol/L of a reactant with coefficient 1 is used up, a product with coefficient 2 increases by 2x. When the reaction moves forward, reactants decrease (−) and products increase (+), and each equilibrium concentration is the initial concentration plus the change: Equilibrium = Initial + Change. To solve an ICE table problem:

  • ICE table – a table that organizes the initial concentrations, the changes in concentration, and the equilibrium concentrations of each substance in a reaction.
  • The changes follow the coefficients of the balanced equation. If x mol/L of a reactant with coefficient 1 is used up, a product with coefficient 2 increases by 2x.
  • Reactants decrease (−), and products increase (+), when the reaction moves forward.
  • Equilibrium = Initial + Change.
  • Steps to solving ICE table problems:
    1. Write the balanced equation and the K expression.
    2. Fill in the Initial row with the starting concentrations.
    3. Fill in the Change row using x and the coefficients: − for reactants, + for products.
    4. Fill in the Equilibrium row: Initial + Change.
    5. Use the known equilibrium concentration to solve for x.
    6. Substitute the equilibrium concentrations into the K expression and solve.
