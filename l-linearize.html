Linearizing Data

When Robert Boyle plotted the pressure of trapped air against its volume, the points fell on a curve. A curved graph is hard to test by eye, because many different curves look alike, while a straight line is easy to check and its slope can be measured. Scientists therefore change one of the variables before plotting so that the graph becomes a straight line, a process called linearization. If y is inversely proportional to x, we plot y against 1x. If the new graph is a straight line with a positive slope that passes through the origin, the inverse relationship is confirmed, and the slope is the constant k, where k = x × y.

  • Linearization – changing the data (for example, plotting 1x instead of x) so that the graph becomes a straight line.
  • If y is inversely proportional to x (y = kx), plot y vs. 1x.
  • If the new graph is a straight line with a positive slope that passes through the origin, the inverse relationship is confirmed, and the slope is the constant k (k = x × y).
Example
For air in a sealed syringe, the graph of pressure vs. volume is a curve. The graph of pressure vs. 1/volume is a straight line that passes through the origin with a slope of 6000 kPa·mL, the constant value of P × V.
