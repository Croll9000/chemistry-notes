Molality

When a solution is heated, it expands, so its volume and therefore its molarity change slightly with temperature. Chemists who study freezing and boiling points need a concentration that stays the same at every temperature. Molality uses the mass of the solvent instead of the volume of the solution, and mass does not change when a solution is heated or cooled. In this section we will discuss molality and how it differs from molarity.

Molality (m)

Molality counts the solute in moles but compares it with the mass of the solvent alone.

  • Molality (m) – the number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent.
  • The unit of molality is mol/kg, written m and read “molal.”
  • Molality uses the mass of the solvent; molarity uses the volume of the whole solution. Molality does not change with temperature.
