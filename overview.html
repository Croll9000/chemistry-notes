What Atoms Are Made Of

Every atom is built from three particles: protons, neutrons and electrons.

Subatomic Particles

Atoms are composed of 3 subatomic particles:

  • Protons – massive, positively charged particles found in the nucleus
  • Neutrons – massive, neutral particles found in the nucleus
  • Electrons – light, negative particles found outside the nucleus

“Massive” here means heavy compared with an electron: a proton or neutron has about 1836 times the mass of an electron. Protons and neutrons have almost the same mass as each other.

An atom of Helium‑4 has 2 protons and 2 neutrons in its nucleus. Its 1st energy level possesses 2 electrons.

Mass and Charge

Particles this small need their own unit of mass. Compare the three particles side by side.

Subatomic Particles: Mass and Charge

When measuring the masses of particles as small as a proton or neutron, we do not use units like grams or kilograms which are very large. Scientists developed a new unit of mass measurement known as a “unified mass unit”, abbreviated with the symbol u.

  • Unified Mass Unit (u) – unit of mass used to measure very small particles. Equal to 112 of the mass of a C‑12 atom

What Holds an Atom Together?

The electrons stay in the atom because of the attraction between opposite charges.

Attraction Between Charges

  • Charged particles push or pull on each other.
    • Opposite charges attract.
    • Like charges repel.
  • The negative electrons are held in the atom by their attraction to the positive protons in the nucleus.
  • The strength of the pull depends on two things:
    • the size of the charges
    • how far apart they are
  • You’ll learn the rule for this (Coulomb’s law) on the Electric Charge & Coulomb’s Law page.
  • Atoms are mostly empty space.
  • If the nucleus were the size of a marble, the whole atom would be bigger than a football stadium.
