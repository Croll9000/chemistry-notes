Conservation Laws

In every chemical reaction, mass, atoms, energy and charge are conserved: they are rearranged or transferred, but never created or destroyed.

Part1

Conservation of Mass

The mass of the reactants equals the mass of the products.

Law of Conservation of Mass

When wood burns, the ashes weigh much less than the log. The “missing” mass did not disappear: it left as carbon dioxide and water vapor. If every product is captured, the total mass never changes.

  • Law of Conservation of Mass – mass (matter) cannot be created or destroyed.
  • In any chemical reaction, the total mass of the reactants must be equal to the total mass of the products.
When combined, the mass of the reactants equals the mass of the products.

Predict Mass in Reactions

Because mass is conserved, a chemist who knows every mass in a reaction but one can calculate the missing mass without measuring it.

  • Since mass can be neither created nor destroyed, we can predict the amount of a substance that is consumed or produced in a chemical reaction.
  • Steps to Calculating a Missing Mass:
    1. Create an algebraic equation by writing the mass of the given reactants and products.
    2. Set the mass of the reactants equal to the mass of the products. Think of the arrow like an “equal sign.”
    3. Let the unknown be x.
    4. Solve for x.

Part2

Conservation of Atoms

The same atoms before and after.

Conservation of Atoms

Mass is conserved because the atoms themselves are conserved. A reaction breaks bonds and forms new ones, but every atom is still there at the end.

  • In any chemical reaction, the total number and type of atoms remains the same before and after a chemical reaction.
    • Although the number of atoms of each element must remain the same, the number of molecules (or moles) does not necessarily need to.
For any chemical reaction, the total number of atoms of each element is the same on the reactants’ and products’ side.
Cause: mass is conserved because atoms are conserved. Every atom of the reactants ends up in the products, and each atom keeps its mass.
Part3

Conservation of Energy

Energy changes form.

Law of Conservation of Energy

A burning candle gives off heat and light, and an instant cold pack pulls heat out of your hand. Neither one creates or destroys energy: it changes form and moves between the chemicals and their surroundings.

  • Law of Conservation of Energy – energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted into different forms.
  • If there is a difference in the potential energy of the reactants and products, that energy will be released or absorbed as light, heat, etc.
  • Ex: when methane burns, the chemical potential energy stored in its bonds is converted into heat and light. The energy given off equals the potential energy lost by the chemicals.

Conservation of Charge

Electrons move, but the total charge stays the same.

Law of Conservation of Charge

Electrons can move from one atom to another during a reaction, but they never appear or disappear. The total charge before a reaction equals the total charge after it.

  • Law of Conservation of Charge – charge cannot be created or destroyed.
    • Charge can be transferred from one object or particle to another as electrons are passed back and forth.
    • In any chemical reaction, the total charge of the reactants must equal the total charge of the products.
Note how the total charge is the same for both the products and the reactants. However, electrons were transferred from iron to copper.
