The Rate of Dissolving

Factors That Affect the Rate of Dissolving

A sugar cube dropped into a glass of iced tea can sit on the bottom for minutes, while a spoonful of granulated sugar stirred into a cup of hot tea disappears in seconds. Both drinks could eventually dissolve the same amount of sugar; what differs is how quickly it happens. The rate of dissolving is how fast a solute dissolves. It is different from solubility, which is how much of a solute can dissolve. It helps to think of solubility as the size of a container and the rate of dissolving as how quickly the container is filled.

A solute dissolves only from its surface, as solvent particles collide with it and pull its particles away. Thus, anything that brings more solvent into contact with the surface, or that makes the collisions more frequent and more forceful, speeds up dissolving:

  • Increasing the temperature makes the solvent particles move faster, so they collide with the solute more often and with more force.
  • Increasing the surface area, by crushing or grinding the solute, exposes more solute particles to the solvent at once.
  • Stirring or shaking carries fresh solvent to the surface of the solute.

The effect of surface area can be larger than it seems. Consider a sugar cube 1 cm on each side, which has a surface area of 6 cm2. Cut it into 1,000 tiny cubes that are each 1 mm on a side, and the total surface area becomes 60 cm2, ten times larger, even though the mass of sugar is the same. Ten times as many sugar molecules are exposed to the water at any moment.

Stirring works for a different reason. Without stirring, the water right next to the solid fills up with dissolved solute and becomes saturated, so dissolving at the surface slows down. Stirring sweeps that layer away and replaces it with fresh solvent. Unlike temperature, stirring and surface area change only how fast a solute dissolves, not how much of it can dissolve. Raising the temperature is the only one of the three factors that changes both the rate and the solubility.

  • The rate of dissolving is how quickly a solute dissolves. It is different from solubility, which is how much solute can dissolve.
  • Factors that increase the rate of dissolving:
    • Increasing the temperature – solvent particles move faster and collide with the solute more often and with more force.
    • Increasing the surface area (crushing or grinding the solute) – more solute particles are exposed to the solvent at once.
    • Stirring or shaking – brings fresh solvent into contact with the solute.
  • Stirring and surface area change only how fast a solute dissolves, not how much can dissolve.
