Avogadro’s Law

A party balloon filled with 0.50 mol of helium at room temperature and normal air pressure holds about 12 L of gas; with 1.00 mol of helium, the same balloon swells to about 24 L. At the same temperature and pressure, the volume of a gas depends on how many particles it contains. In this section, we will discuss Avogadro’s law, the relationship between the amount of a gas and its volume.

Avogadro’s Law

When two molecules of octane burn in a car engine, 25 molecules of oxygen gas become 16 molecules of carbon dioxide and 18 molecules of water vapor. The amount of gas changes during the reaction, so the volume the gas takes up changes as well.

  • The combined gas law works under the assumption that the number of gas particles in a sample remains constant. However, during many chemical reactions, gases can be produced or used up in addition to changes in temperature, pressure and volume.
  • The volume of a gas varies directly with the moles of gas particles in a sample, assuming temperature and pressure are held constant.
    • Increasing the number of gas particles in a sample will increase the volume.
    • Decreasing the number of gas particles in a sample will decrease the volume.
  • Avogadro’s Law – the volume of a gas is directly proportional to the number of moles of gas particles in a sample when temperature and pressure are held constant.
V1n1=V2n2
V1 – initial volume (m3 or L)n1 – initial number of moles (mol)V2 – final volume (m3 or L)n2 – final number of moles (mol)
Example
Consider blowing into a balloon. When the amount of gas particles in the balloon increases, its volume increases. When the balloon is deflated, the amount of gas particles decreases, and therefore the volume decreases.
  • A graph of volume against moles of gas is a straight line through the origin (a direct relationship).
  • Avogadro’s law also explains why equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of particles, and why 1 mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L at STP (see Unit 6: Molar Volume).

Solving Avogadro’s Law Problems

An adult’s lungs hold about 6 L of air when completely full, roughly 0.24 mol of gas at body temperature and normal air pressure. Because the temperature and pressure inside the lungs stay nearly constant, the volume of air breathed in is proportional to the number of moles of air. In this section, we will solve Avogadro’s law problems.

Solving Problems

Avogadro’s law problems give three of the four values V1, n1, V2 and n2. The temperature and pressure are held constant, so they are not part of the calculation.

  • Steps for solving gas law problems:
    1. Read the question and list all given variables and the unknown.
    2. If necessary, convert all temperatures to kelvin.
    3. Write down the gas law.
    4. Substitute the variables into the equation and solve for the unknown algebraically.
    5. Write the final answer with units.
  • The two volumes must have the same unit.
