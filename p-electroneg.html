Electronegativity

Electronegativity is a measure of an atom’s attraction for the electrons in a chemical bond. It follows the same pattern as ionization energy.

Attracting Electrons

Atoms in a bond attract the shared electrons.

Electronegativity

When two atoms form a chemical bond, they do not always share their electrons equally. Some atoms pull on bonding electrons more strongly than others, and this difference affects properties such as whether a substance dissolves in water and how high its boiling point is.

  • Electronegativity – an atom’s ability to attract electrons (in a chemical bond).
    • Unit – none (electronegativity is a relative scale)
  • Electronegativity is measured on a scale from 0 to 4.
    • 0 is the least electronegative: the weakest attraction for electrons.
    • 4 is the most electronegative: the strongest attraction for electrons.
  • Fluorine is the most electronegative element (3.98).
  • Cesium and francium are the least electronegative elements (about 0.7–0.8).
  • Most noble gases have no electronegativity value, because they rarely form bonds.
Chlorine is more electronegative than hydrogen, so it has a stronger attraction for electrons. When a chemical bond forms between the two, chlorine has a higher concentration of electron density around itself.

Place two elements next to each other. The atom with the higher electronegativity pulls the shared pair of electrons toward itself.

The Trends

Electronegativity across a period and down a group.

Electronegativity in Periods

  • The greater the nuclear charge (# of protons) in an atom, the stronger its attraction for electrons and the higher its electronegativity.
  • As you move from left to right across a period, electronegativity increases.
    • The nuclear charge (# of protons) increases.
Cause: the nuclear charge increases across a period while the number of energy levels stays the same, so the nucleus attracts bonding electrons more strongly.

Electronegativity in Groups

  • Recall that each new period adds an energy level to the atom.
    • Moving down a group, atoms increase in size.
  • The larger an atom is, the farther its nucleus is from the electrons of a neighboring atom.
    • This reduces its ability to attract those electrons.
  • As you move down a group, electronegativity decreases.
    • The distance between the nucleus and other atoms’ electrons increases.
Cause: each step down a group adds an energy level, so the nucleus is farther from the bonding electrons and attracts them less strongly.

Electronegativity Trends

  • Electronegativity increases from left to right across a period.
  • Electronegativity decreases from top to bottom down a group.
  • The most electronegative elements are at the top right (F, O, N, Cl).
  • Nonmetals have high electronegativities, and metals have low electronegativities.
Across a period: increasing nuclear charge, same number of energy levels → electronegativity increases.
Down a group: more energy levels, greater distance from the nucleus → electronegativity decreases.

Build atoms across a period by adding protons and electrons, or down a group by adding energy levels. Compare the electronegativity of each atom.
