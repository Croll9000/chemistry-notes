Ground State and Excited States

Electrons normally occupy the lowest available energy levels. When an electron absorbs energy, it can move to a higher energy level.

Ground State

The colors of fireworks and the glow of neon signs are produced by electrons moving between energy levels. To understand how atoms give off light, we first need to understand where electrons are found under normal conditions.

Under normal conditions, electrons inhabit the lowest energy levels possible within an atom. This state is known as the ground state.

  • Ground state – Lowest energy electron configuration of an atom.
The electrons in Magnesium are normally found in the configuration 2-8-2. This is the lowest energy state for the electrons and known as the ground state.

Excited State

Electrons can absorb energy from their surroundings in the form of light or heat. After absorbing this energy, they move into a higher energy level. This is referred to as an excited state.

  • Excited State – A configuration in which electrons occupy higher energy levels than the ground state.
Notice how after absorbing light (energy) an electron in the 2nd shell “jumped” into the 3rd shell (gold electron). The dotted circle shows the spot it left. This means that the atom/electron are in an excited state. They possess more energy than they did in the ground state.

An excited state has the same total number of electrons as the ground state. Only their arrangement changes: at least one electron is in a higher level than it needs to be.

Part2

Absorbing and Emitting Energy

Electrons absorb energy to move up and release energy as light when they fall back. With enough energy, the electron is removed from the atom.

Electron Transitions

Electrons that absorb energy and jump into an excited state do not remain there forever. Eventually, the electron returns to the ground state and when it does, emits energy in the form of light.

Steps:

  1. An electron absorbs energy and jumps from the ground state to the excited state.
  2. The electron returns to the ground state from the excited state and releases energy in the form of light.
Step 1: Energy is absorbed and an electron jumps from the ground state (low energy level) to the excited state (higher energy level).
Step 2: The electron returns to the ground state from the excited state. In the process, it emits energy in the form of light.
  • An electron can only absorb an amount of energy that exactly matches the difference between two energy levels.
    • Each element has its own levels, so each element needs its own specific amounts.

Ionization

Sometimes an electron can absorb so much energy that it doesn’t just jump up in energy level, it leaves the atom completely. If this happens, the atom loses an electron and acquires a net positive charge. This process is called ionization.

  • Ionization – process by which an atom loses electrons and acquires a positive charge.
  • The smallest amount of energy that removes the outermost electron is called the ionization energy.
The outermost electron in this lithium atom absorbs enough energy to become free from the atom. This leaves the lithium with one more proton than electrons and a net positive charge, Li+.
