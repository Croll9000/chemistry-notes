Ground State and Excited States

Ground State

A ball sitting at the bottom of a staircase is in its most stable position. It can be carried up to a higher step, but left alone it will always end up back at the bottom. Electrons behave in a similar way. Under normal conditions, an atom’s electrons fill the lowest energy levels available, in the order given by its shell notation, because that arrangement has the least energy.

The ground state is the lowest energy electron configuration of an atom. Sodium’s ground state is 2-8-1, and it is the configuration shown for each element in shell notation. An atom in its ground state is stable, and it will stay that way unless energy is added to it.

Under normal conditions, electrons inhabit the lowest energy levels possible within an atom. This state is known as the ground state.

  • Ground state – Lowest energy electron configuration of an atom.
The electrons in Magnesium are normally found in the configuration 2-8-2. This is the lowest energy state for the electrons and known as the ground state.

Excited State

Heat a few grains of table salt in a flame, and the flame suddenly turns a bright yellow-orange. The heat gives some of the electrons in the sodium atoms extra energy, enough to lift them into higher energy levels than they normally occupy. For a brief moment, those atoms are not in their ground state.

An excited state is a configuration in which electrons occupy higher energy levels than the ground state. A sodium atom with the configuration 2-7-2 is in an excited state: it still has 11 electrons, but one electron from the second level has jumped up to the third. An excited state has the same total number of electrons as the ground state; only their arrangement is different.

An excited state can be recognized from its shell notation. If an inner level holds fewer electrons than it should in the ground state while a higher level holds more, an electron has been moved up, and the atom is excited. Excited states are unstable, and the electron soon falls back down.

Electrons can absorb energy from their surroundings in the form of light or heat. After absorbing this energy, they move into a higher energy level. This is referred to as an excited state.

  • Excited State – A configuration in which electrons occupy higher energy levels than the ground state.
Notice how after absorbing light (energy) an electron in the 2nd shell “jumped” into the 3rd shell (gold electron). The dotted circle shows the spot it left. This means that the atom/electron are in an excited state. They possess more energy than they did in the ground state.

An excited state has the same total number of electrons as the ground state. Only their arrangement changes: at least one electron is in a higher level than it needs to be.

Absorbing and Emitting Energy

Electron Transitions

Older streetlights glow orange-yellow, and the color comes from sodium. Inside each lamp, electricity excites the electrons in sodium vapor, and as the electrons fall back to their ground state, they release their extra energy as light with a wavelength of 589 nm. Every sodium atom gives off exactly the same color, because the energy levels in every sodium atom are exactly the same.

An electron absorbs energy and jumps from the ground state to an excited state. It does not stay there for long. When it returns to the ground state, it releases energy in the form of light. Additionally, an electron can only absorb an amount of energy that exactly matches the difference between two energy levels, just as a person on a staircase can climb exactly one step or exactly two, but never one and a half.

Since each element has its own set of energy levels, the differences between them are unique to that element, so each element absorbs and gives off its own specific amounts of energy. Ionization

Many household smoke detectors contain a tiny amount of the element americium, which gives off radiation that knocks electrons off the molecules of air inside a small chamber. The charged particles that are left allow a small electric current to flow through the air. When smoke drifts into the chamber, it gets in the way of the current, and the alarm sounds.

If an electron absorbs a small amount of energy, it jumps to a higher energy level. If it absorbs enough energy, it can escape from the atom completely. Ionization is the process by which an atom loses electrons and acquires a positive charge. The smallest amount of energy that removes the outermost electron from an atom is referred to as the ionization energy.

The outermost electron is always the one removed first, since it is the farthest from the nucleus and the most weakly attracted to it. Atoms whose outer electrons are held weakly, such as sodium, have low ionization energies and form positive ions easily.

Sometimes an electron can absorb so much energy that it doesn’t just jump up in energy level, it leaves the atom completely. If this happens, the atom loses an electron and acquires a net positive charge. This process is called ionization.

  • Ionization – process by which an atom loses electrons and acquires a positive charge.
  • The smallest amount of energy that removes the outermost electron is called the ionization energy.
The outermost electron in this lithium atom absorbs enough energy to become free from the atom. This leaves the lithium with one more proton than electrons and a net positive charge, Li+.
