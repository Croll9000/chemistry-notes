Groups & Periods

An element’s period (row) equals its number of energy levels. Its group (column) determines its number of valence electrons, which determines how it reacts.

Rows and Columns

The period number equals the number of energy levels. Elements in a group have the same number of valence electrons.

Periods

The Periodic Table is arranged much like a calendar. Just as the days of the week repeat in each row of a calendar, the properties of elements repeat in each row of the table. This repetition is the reason it is called the periodic table.

  • The table is organized into rows called periods and columns called groups.
  • Period – horizontal row on the periodic table.
    • The period number is equal to the number of energy levels (shells) in the atom.

Groups

  • Group – vertical column on the periodic table.
    • Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons.
    • Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties.

Groups and Valence Electrons

  • Elements in the same group possess the same number of valence electrons.
    • This gives them similar chemical properties.
  • You can read the number of valence electrons from the group number.
    • Groups 1 and 2: the group number (1 or 2).
    • Groups 13–18: subtract 10 from the group number (group 15 → 5 valence electrons).
    • Helium is the exception in group 18: it has 2 valence electrons, a full first shell.
  • The transition metals (groups 3–12) have a variable number of valence electrons.

Valence Electrons and Reactivity

Atoms react to reach a full outer shell.

Reactivity of Elements

Sodium reacts violently with water, while gold can sit for thousands of years without tarnishing. The difference in how readily elements react is explained by their valence electrons.

  • Some elements are more reactive than others.
    • The reactivity of an element is determined by its number of valence electrons.
  • Most atoms are stable when they have 8 electrons in their valence shell.
    • Octet – 8 electrons in an atom’s valence shell.
    • Elements in period 1 (hydrogen and helium) only need 2 electrons to fill their valence shell.
  • Elements that are closest to having a full octet, by losing or gaining electrons, are the most reactive.
    • Group 1 metals lose 1 electron.
    • Group 17 nonmetals gain 1 electron.
  • An atom with a full octet is unreactive.
    • The elements in group 18, called the noble gases, have full octets.
