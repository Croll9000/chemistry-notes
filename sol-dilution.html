Dilutions

Hospitals and laboratories buy hydrochloric acid at about 12 M and dilute it with water to the strength they need. Adding water does not change how many moles of acid are in the container; it only spreads them through a larger volume. That single idea is enough to calculate the concentration or the volume of any diluted solution.

Dilution

Dilution

Hydrochloric acid is sold to schools as a 12 M solution, far too concentrated to use safely in most labs. Before class, a teacher measures out a small volume of the concentrated acid and adds it to water to make a larger volume of a weaker solution, such as 1.0 M. No acid is added or taken away in the process; the same number of moles is simply spread through a larger volume. Adding solvent to a solution to lower its concentration is referred to as dilution.

If we analyze the definition of molarity, it becomes clear how to calculate a dilution. Rearranging M = nV gives n = MV, so the moles of solute in any solution equal its molarity times its volume. During a dilution, the moles of solute do not change, so the moles before must equal the moles after:

n1 = n2 so M1V1 = M2V2

Molarity and volume are inversely proportional during a dilution: doubling the volume halves the molarity, and increasing the volume tenfold lowers the molarity to one-tenth. The two volumes may be in milliliters or liters, as long as both are in the same unit, because the units cancel. The equation gives the final volume of the solution, so the volume of water to add is the final volume minus the starting volume.

When diluting an acid, the acid is always added to the water, and never the water to the acid. Mixing a concentrated acid with water releases a large amount of heat. Pouring a little acid into a lot of water spreads that heat through the water safely, while pouring water into the acid can make the small amount of water boil instantly and spatter acid out of the container.

  • Dilution – adding solvent to a solution to lower its concentration.
  • The moles of solute do not change: M1V1 = M2V2
  • The volume of water to add is the final volume minus the starting volume.
