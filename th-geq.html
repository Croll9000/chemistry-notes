Free Energy and Equilibrium

A ball rolls downhill and comes to rest at the bottom of a valley. A reaction mixture behaves the same way: it always changes in the direction that lowers its free energy, and it stops changing at the lowest point. The equilibrium mixture is therefore the mixture with the lowest free energy. At equilibrium, ΔG = 0, because neither direction lowers the free energy, so there is no net change. The standard free energy change, ΔG°, is related to the equilibrium constant: ΔG° < 0 means K > 1, so products are favored, and ΔG° > 0 means K < 1, so reactants are favored.

  • A reaction mixture always changes in the direction that lowers its free energy.
  • At equilibrium, ΔG = 0: neither direction lowers the free energy, so there is no net change.
  • The standard free energy change, ΔG°, is related to the equilibrium constant:
ΔG°=−RT ln K
R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)T – temperature (K)K – equilibrium constant
  • ΔG° < 0 means K > 1 (products favored); ΔG° > 0 means K < 1 (reactants favored).
