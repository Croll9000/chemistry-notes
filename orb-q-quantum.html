Quantum Numbers

Every electron in an atom is described by four quantum numbers: its energy level (n), its sublevel (ℓ), its orbital (mℓ) and its spin (ms). No two electrons in an atom share all four.

Quantum Numbers

An iron atom has 26 electrons, and every one of them can be told apart from the other 25. Just as a mailing address narrows a location down to a state, a city, a street and a house number, four numbers narrow an electron down to its energy level, its sublevel, its orbital and its spin. These numbers come from the quantum mechanical model of the atom, the same model that gives the shapes of s, p, d and f orbitals. In this section, we will discuss the four quantum numbers and what each one tells us about an electron.

Quantum Numbers

In the Bohr model, an electron is described only by the energy level it occupies. The quantum mechanical model describes each electron more completely. It places the electron in an energy level, a sublevel within that energy level, a single orbital within that sublevel, and gives the direction of its spin.

  • Quantum Numbers – a set of four numbers that describes the energy level, sublevel, orbital and spin of an electron in an atom.
  • Principal Quantum Number (n) – the quantum number that gives the main energy level (shell) of an electron.
    • n can be any whole number: 1, 2, 3, 4 and so on.
    • The larger the value of n, the farther the electron is from the nucleus on average and the higher its energy.
    • Energy level n can hold up to 2n2 electrons.
    • The principal quantum number of an atom’s valence electrons matches its period (row) on the periodic table. Sodium is in period 3, so its valence electron has n = 3.
  • Angular Momentum Quantum Number (ℓ) – the quantum number that gives the sublevel of an electron and the shape of its orbital.
    • ℓ can be any whole number from 0 to n − 1.
    • Each value of ℓ is a sublevel: ℓ = 0 is s, ℓ = 1 is p, ℓ = 2 is d and ℓ = 3 is f.
    • Energy level n has n sublevels. For example, energy level 3 has three sublevels: 3s, 3p and 3d.
  • Magnetic Quantum Number (mℓ) – the quantum number that gives the orientation of an orbital in space.
    • mℓ can be any whole number from −ℓ to +ℓ, including 0.
    • Each value of mℓ is one orbital, so a sublevel has 2ℓ + 1 orbitals: s has 1, p has 3, d has 5 and f has 7.
    • For example, the three orbitals of a p sublevel (mℓ = −1, 0 and +1) point along the three axes in space: x, y and z.
  • Spin Quantum Number (ms) – the quantum number that gives the spin of an electron.
    • ms can only be +½ (spin up, ↑) or −½ (spin down, ↓). An electron cannot have a spin of zero.
    • Each orbital holds at most two electrons, and they must have opposite spins.

Writing Quantum Numbers

The two electrons in a filled 1s orbital of a helium atom share the same energy level, the same sublevel and the same orbital, so the first three quantum numbers of both are (1, 0, 0). They can only be told apart by their spins, +½ and −½. This is the Pauli exclusion principle written in quantum numbers. In this section, we will discuss how to write the set of quantum numbers for an electron from an orbital diagram.

The Pauli Exclusion Principle and Quantum Numbers

An orbital diagram already contains all four quantum numbers. The energy level and sublevel come from the label of the sublevel, the orbital comes from which box the electron is in, and the spin comes from the direction of its arrow.

  • No two electrons in the same atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers. This is another way of stating the Pauli exclusion principle.
  • To label the orbitals in a sublevel, write the values of mℓ under the boxes from −ℓ on the left to +ℓ on the right. For a p sublevel, the boxes are −1, 0 and +1.
  • In these notes, electrons fill each sublevel from left to right, and the first electron in each orbital is drawn spin up (+½).
  • Steps for writing the quantum numbers of an electron:
    1. Find the sublevel of the electron. The number is n.
    2. Convert the sublevel letter to ℓ: s = 0, p = 1, d = 2, f = 3.
    3. Find the orbital (box) that holds the electron. Its label is mℓ.
    4. Find the spin from the arrow: up is +½ and down is −½.
    5. Write the set in order: (n, ℓ, mℓ, ms).
