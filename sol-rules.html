Solubility of Ionic Compounds

Ionic compounds that contain a group 1 ion, ammonium, acetate, chlorate, hydrogen carbonate or nitrate are soluble in water.

Ions That Form Aqueous Solutions

Selected Ions Forming Aqueous Solutions

Much of Long Island gets its drinking water from groundwater stored in the sand and gravel beneath it. Over the past several decades, the amount of nitrate in that groundwater has risen, much of it from lawn fertilizers and septic systems. Nitrate is hard to keep out of the water supply for a simple chemical reason: every compound containing the nitrate ion dissolves in water, so rain carries nitrate down through the soil instead of leaving it behind as a solid.

Chemists have tested the solubility of thousands of ionic compounds, and a few ions turn out to form soluble compounds with every other ion. These ions are listed on the Reference Table:

  • Group 1 metal ions (Li+, Na+, K+, etc.)
  • ammonium, NH4+
  • acetate, C2H3O2−
  • chlorate, ClO3−
  • hydrogen carbonate, HCO3−
  • nitrate, NO3−

Since each of these ions always dissolves, it makes sense that an ionic compound that contains at least one of these ions is soluble, and it is written with (aq). Only one of the two ions needs to be on the list. Sodium carbonate, for example, is soluble because it contains the sodium ion, even though most carbonates are not. If a compound contains none of these ions, the table cannot decide, and a question must give its solubility, for example by stating that “phosphates are insoluble with most metals.”

The ions on the list are also why so many reactions in solution can be predicted. When two soluble compounds are mixed, their ions can trade partners. If one of the new combinations is insoluble, it forms a solid that separates from the solution, referred to as a precipitate. Consider solutions of potassium iodide and lead(II) nitrate, both clear and colorless. When they are mixed, a bright yellow precipitate of lead(II) iodide forms at once, while the potassium nitrate stays dissolved, as it must, since it contains two ions from the list.

To decide whether an ionic compound is soluble:

  1. Identify the two ions in the compound.
  2. Check the Reference Table for either ion.
  3. If either ion is listed, the compound is soluble and is written with (aq).
  4. If neither ion is listed, use the information given in the question.
