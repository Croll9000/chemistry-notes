Solubility of Ionic Compounds

Ionic compounds that contain a group 1 ion, ammonium, acetate, chlorate, hydrogen carbonate or nitrate are soluble in water.

Ions That Form Aqueous Solutions

Before an X-ray of the stomach, a patient drinks a thick white mixture of barium sulfate in water. Barium ions are poisonous, but barium sulfate is so insoluble that almost none of it dissolves, so it passes through the body safely while blocking X-rays. Whether an ionic compound dissolves can make the difference between a medicine and a poison. In this section we will discuss how to use the Reference Table to decide whether an ionic compound is soluble in water.

Selected Ions Forming Aqueous Solutions

The Reference Table lists the ions that form soluble compounds with every other ion. If a compound contains one of these ions, it dissolves in water.

  • The Reference Table lists the ions that always form aqueous solutions:
    • Group 1 metal ions (Li+, Na+, K+, etc.)
    • ammonium, NH4+
    • acetate, C2H3O2−
    • chlorate, ClO3−
    • hydrogen carbonate, HCO3−
    • nitrate, NO3−
  • An ionic compound that contains at least one of these ions is soluble and is written with (aq).
  • If a compound contains none of these ions, the table does not decide. A question must give its solubility, for example “phosphates are insoluble with most metals.”
  • Precipitate – an insoluble solid that forms and separates from a solution.
  • Steps to deciding whether an ionic compound is soluble:
    1. Identify the two ions in the compound.
    2. Check the Reference Table for either ion.
    3. If either ion is listed, the compound is soluble and is written with (aq).
    4. If neither ion is listed, use the information given in the question.
