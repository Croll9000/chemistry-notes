Zeroth Law: Thermal Equilibrium

The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics

A nurse can read your temperature only because the thermometer and your body end up at the same temperature. Thermodynamics is the study of energy, heat and work, and how they change from one form to another, and its four laws describe how energy moves and changes. Heat flows from a hotter object to a colder one until their temperatures are equal, a state called thermal equilibrium, in which there is no net flow of heat between the objects. The zeroth law of thermodynamics states that if two objects are each in thermal equilibrium with a third object, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This is why a thermometer works: the thermometer reaches the temperature of the object it touches.

  • Thermodynamics – the study of energy, heat and work, and how they change from one form to another.
  • Thermal equilibrium – the state in which two objects in contact are at the same temperature, so there is no net flow of heat between them.
  • Zeroth law of thermodynamics – if two objects are each in thermal equilibrium with a third object, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
  • This is why a thermometer works: the thermometer reaches the temperature of the object it touches.

First Law: Conservation of Energy

The First Law of Thermodynamics

A campfire turns the chemical energy stored in wood into heat and light, and no energy is created or destroyed along the way. To track energy, chemists divide the world into the system, the part of the universe being studied, and the surroundings, everything outside the system. The first law of thermodynamics states that energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only change form or be transferred, so the total energy of the universe is constant. Energy lost by the system is gained by the surroundings, and energy gained by the system is lost by the surroundings. The energy of a system changes by heat (q) and by work (w).

  • System – the part of the universe being studied, such as the chemicals in a reaction.
  • Surroundings – everything outside the system.
  • First law of thermodynamics – energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only change form or be transferred. The total energy of the universe is constant.
  • Energy lost by the system is gained by the surroundings, and energy gained by the system is lost by the surroundings.
  • The energy of a system changes by heat (q) and work (w):

Part3

Second Law: Entropy Increases

The Second Law of Thermodynamics

An ice cube left on a table always melts, and a warm drink always cools down; nobody has ever seen a puddle on a warm table freeze into an ice cube by itself. Energy and matter tend to spread out. Entropy (S) is a measure of the disorder or randomness of a system, or how spread out its energy is. The second law of thermodynamics states that in any spontaneous process, the total entropy of the universe increases. This is why heat flows on its own only from a hotter object to a colder one. Particles spread out because there are far more spread-out arrangements than gathered ones; spreading out is not forced, but it is overwhelmingly likely.

  • Entropy (S) – a measure of the disorder or randomness of a system, or how spread out its energy is.
  • Second law of thermodynamics – in any spontaneous process, the total entropy of the universe (system + surroundings) increases.
  • Heat flows on its own only from a hotter object to a colder one.
  • Particles spread out because there are far more spread-out arrangements than gathered ones. Spreading out is not forced; it is overwhelmingly likely.

Third Law: Absolute Zero

The Third Law of Thermodynamics

Liquid helium, at 4 K, is one of the coldest substances in a laboratory, but no laboratory has ever reached 0 K. As a substance gets colder, its particles move less and less. The third law of thermodynamics states that the entropy of a perfect crystal at absolute zero is zero. At 0 K the particles of a perfect crystal are perfectly ordered, with only one possible arrangement, and as the temperature rises, the particles vibrate more and the entropy increases.

  • Third law of thermodynamics – the entropy of a perfect crystal at absolute zero (0 K) is zero.
  • At 0 K the particles of a perfect crystal are perfectly ordered, with only one possible arrangement.
  • As the temperature rises, the particles vibrate more and the entropy increases.
