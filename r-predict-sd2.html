More Synthesis and Decomposition Patterns

The emergency oxygen masks above airplane seats are supplied by heating sodium chlorate, NaClO₃, which decomposes into sodium chloride and oxygen gas. Compounds that contain the same polyatomic ion break down in the same pattern: every metal chlorate releases oxygen and leaves a metal chloride. In this section, we will predict the products of synthesis and decomposition reactions that involve compounds.

Synthesis with Compounds

Synthesis reactions are not limited to elements. Lime (calcium oxide) combines with water to form calcium hydroxide, a reaction used to make mortar and plaster. Compounds such as metal oxides and nonmetal oxides combine with water in predictable patterns.

  • Synthesis reactions that involve compounds depend on the specific types of substances combining.
  • Steps to Predicting Synthesis Reactions (Compounds):
    1. Identify the types of substances combining.
    2. Use the table to find the product. If the product is ionic, criss-cross the charges of the ions to determine its formula and simplify if necessary.
    3. Balance the equation.

Decomposition of Compounds with Polyatomic Ions

Heating limestone (calcium carbonate) produces lime and carbon dioxide, a reaction used on a huge scale to make cement. Compounds that contain polyatomic ions break down in patterns that depend on the polyatomic ion, so the products can be predicted from the type of compound.

  • Decomposition reactions of compounds that are not binary depend on the specific class of compound breaking down.
  • Steps to Predicting Decomposition Reactions (Compounds):
    1. Identify the type of compound breaking down.
    2. Use the table to find the products. If a product is ionic, criss-cross the charges of the ions to determine its formula and simplify if necessary.
    3. Balance the equation.
