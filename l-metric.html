Base Units and Prefixes

A metric unit is a base unit with a prefix in front of it.

The Metric System

Before the 1790s, units of measurement varied from town to town, which made trade and science difficult. The metric system was developed in France to replace them with a single, logical system. Today it is used by scientists in every country and in everyday life by nearly every nation in the world.

  • In science we always use the Metric System – a system of measurement based on factors of 10.
  • Every metric unit is made of a base unit, sometimes with a prefix in front of it.
    • Base units you will use: meter (m), gram (g), liter (L), second (s), joule (J), mole (mol)
    • Prefix – a word part placed in front of a base unit that multiplies it by a power of 10.
      • Ex: a kilometer is 1000 (103) meters.
  • Since the prefixes mean the same thing for every unit, if you know them for meters, you know them for grams, liters and joules too.

Metric Prefixes

Quantities in chemistry range from the mass of a truck to the mass of a single atom. Writing every measurement in base units would produce numbers with many zeros. Prefixes allow us to scale a unit up or down by factors of 10 to fit the size of what is being measured.

Your Reference Table lists four Selected Metric Prefixes (kilo-, milli-, micro- and nano-). Centi- is not on the Reference Table, but it is used on every centimeter ruler, so learn it too.

The prefix symbol is a factor. To read a prefixed unit, replace the prefix with its factor:
5.2 km = 5.2 × 103 m = 5200 m
250 mg = 250 × 10−3 g = 0.250 g
Careful with the letter m: m by itself is the base unit meter, but m in front of another unit is the prefix milli- (mL, mg, mm). The symbol for micro- is the Greek letter μ (“mu”).

Converting To and From Base Units

Multiply by the factor to get to the base unit; divide by it to get away from the base unit.

Converting To and From Base Units

  • It is often necessary to convert between a base unit and a prefixed unit.
  • To do so we use the factor of the prefix: how many of the smaller unit fit in one of the larger unit.
    • To convert from a larger unit to a smaller unit, you multiply by the factor. (You need more of the smaller unit.)
    • To convert from a smaller unit to a larger unit, you divide by the factor. (You need fewer of the larger unit.)
Larger unitSmaller unitHow many smaller units fit in one larger unit
1 kmm103 m
1 mcm102 cm
1 mmm103 mm
1 mμm106 μm
1 mnm109 nm

Your Reference Table writes milli- as 1 mm = 10−3 m. That says the same thing as 1 m = 103 mm.

Larger → smaller: multiply

Ex: How many meters are equal to 35 kilometers?

35 km × 103 = 35,000 m

Smaller → larger: divide

Ex: What quantity of heat, in kilojoules, is equal to 200. joules?

200. J ÷ 103 = 0.200 kJ

Check your answer: there should be more of a smaller unit and fewer of a larger unit. 35 km is a lot of meters (35,000), but 200 J is only a fraction of a kilojoule (0.200).

To convert between two prefixed units (like mg to kg), go through the base unit first. The Dimensional Analysis page shows a step-by-step method that works for every conversion.
