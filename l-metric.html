Base Units and Prefixes

The Metric System

Before the French Revolution, France had hundreds of different units of measurement, and the length of a “foot” or the size of a “pound” could change from one town to the next. In the 1790s, French scientists replaced them with a single system built on factors of 10, so that converting between units would be as easy as moving a decimal point. Today, almost every country in the world uses the metric system for everyday life, and every country uses it for science.

In science, we always use the metric system. Every metric unit is made of a base unit, such as the meter (m), gram (g), liter (L), second (s), joule (J) or mole (mol), sometimes with a prefix in front of it that multiplies the unit by a power of 10. A kilometer, for instance, is 1000 (103) meters.

Metric System – a system of measurement based on factors of 10.

Prefix – a word part placed in front of a base unit that multiplies it by a power of 10.

What makes the system so useful is that the prefixes mean the same thing for every unit. Kilo- means 1000 whether it is in front of meters, grams, liters or joules, so once we know the prefixes for one unit, we know them for all of them. Compare this with the U.S. customary system, where 12 inches make a foot, 3 feet make a yard and 16 ounces make a pound.

Metric Prefixes

The names of the metric prefixes come from Greek and Latin words. Kilo- comes from the Greek word for “thousand,” while centi- and milli- come from the Latin words for “hundred” and “thousand,” used for the fractions a hundredth and a thousandth. Each prefix stands for a factor, a power of 10, and we can read any prefixed unit by replacing the prefix with its factor.

Your Reference Table lists four Selected Metric Prefixes (kilo-, milli-, micro- and nano-). Centi- is not on the Reference Table, but it is used on every centimeter ruler, so learn it too.

The prefix symbol is a factor. To read a prefixed unit, replace the prefix with its factor:
5.2 km = 5.2 × 103 m = 5200 m
250 mg = 250 × 10−3 g = 0.250 g
Careful with the letter m: m by itself is the base unit meter, but m in front of another unit is the prefix milli- (mL, mg, mm). The symbol for micro- is the Greek letter μ (“mu”).

Converting To and From Base Units

Converting To and From Base Units

It is often necessary to convert between a base unit and a prefixed unit, and to do this we use the factor of the prefix: how many of the smaller unit fit in one of the larger unit. To convert from a larger unit to a smaller unit, we multiply by the factor, since more of the smaller unit are needed to describe the same amount. To convert from a smaller unit to a larger unit, we divide by the factor, since fewer of the larger unit are needed.

Consider a 5-kilometer race. Converting from kilometers to meters means switching from a large unit to a small one, so the number gets larger. Since each kilometer holds 1000 meters, the race is 5 × 1000 = 5000 m long. Converting back means switching to the larger unit, so the number gets smaller.

A good check is that the answer should always have more of a smaller unit and fewer of a larger unit.

Your Reference Table writes milli- as 1 mm = 10−3 m. That says the same thing as 1 m = 103 mm.

Larger → smaller: multiply

Ex: How many meters are equal to 35 kilometers?

35 km × 103 = 35,000 m

Smaller → larger: divide

Ex: What quantity of heat, in kilojoules, is equal to 200. joules?

200. J103 = 0.200 kJ

Check your answer: there should be more of a smaller unit and fewer of a larger unit. 35 km is a lot of meters (35,000), but 200 J is only a fraction of a kilojoule (0.200).
