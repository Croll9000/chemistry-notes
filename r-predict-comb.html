Predicting Combustion

Combustion Reactions

Burning one gallon of gasoline releases about 8.9 kg of carbon dioxide. Natural gas (methane), propane, butane and gasoline (octane) are all hydrocarbons, molecules containing only hydrogen and carbon, and burning them releases the energy that heats homes and moves cars. A combustion reaction is a chemical reaction in which a hydrocarbon reacts with oxygen (O2) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O). Combustion reactions are exothermic, releasing heat, and their products are always the same: every carbon atom in the fuel ends up in CO2, and every hydrogen atom ends up in H2O. A combustion equation is balanced with the same steps as any other equation.

  • Combustion Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a hydrocarbon (a molecule containing only hydrogen and carbon) reacts with oxygen (O2) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
    • Combustion reactions are exothermic, meaning they release heat.
  • Predicting the products is always the same: CxHy + O2 → CO2 + H2O.
