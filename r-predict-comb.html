Combustion Reactions

Natural gas (methane), propane, butane and gasoline (octane) are all hydrocarbons. Burning them releases the energy that heats homes and moves cars.

  • Combustion Reaction – a chemical reaction in which a hydrocarbon (a molecule containing only hydrogen and carbon) reacts with oxygen (O2) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
    • Combustion reactions are exothermic, meaning they release heat.
  • Predicting the products is always the same: CxHy + O2 → CO2 + H2O.
  • Balance a combustion equation with the same steps as any other equation (see Balancing Chemical Reactions).
