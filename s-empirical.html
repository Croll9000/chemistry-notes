Empirical and Molecular Formulas

Glucose, C6H12O6, and formaldehyde, CH2O, contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms in the same 1 : 2 : 1 ratio, yet one is a sugar and the other is a toxic gas. A glucose molecule simply contains 6 times as many atoms of each element. A molecular formula indicates exactly how many atoms of each element are present in a compound. An empirical formula indicates only the simplest, lowest whole-number ratio of the elements in a compound, so glucose and formaldehyde have the same empirical formula, CH2O. When chemists discover a new substance, the first thing a lab can usually measure is how much of each element it contains. Lab data often gives the empirical formula but not the molecular formula.

  • A molecular formula indicates exactly how many atoms of each element are present in a compound.
    • Another type of formula, known as an empirical formula, conveys only the simplest ratio of elements in a compound.
    • Data collected in a lab will often give information about the empirical but not the molecular formula.
    • Molecular Formula – indicates how many atoms of each element are present in a compound.
    • Empirical Formula – indicates the simplest (lowest whole-number) ratio of elements in a compound.
Example
Glucose, C6H12O6: dividing every subscript by 6 gives CH2O (empirical formula).

Molecular Formula → Empirical Formula

An empirical formula is found by reducing the subscripts of a molecular formula to the smallest whole-number ratio. Dividing every subscript in C6H12O6 by 6 gives CH2O. The steps for finding the empirical formula from a molecular formula are:

  1. Find the largest number that divides every subscript evenly.
  2. Divide every subscript by that number.

If the only number that divides every subscript evenly is 1, the molecular formula is already an empirical formula.

Empirical Formula → Molecular Formula

A molecular formula is always a whole-number multiple of its empirical formula, and the molar mass of the compound tells us which multiple. The mass of the empirical formula CH2O is 30.0 g/mol, and the molar mass of glucose is 180.2 g/mol, so a glucose molecule contains 6 empirical formula units. The steps for finding the molecular formula from an empirical formula are:

  1. Determine the mass of the empirical formula.
  2. Divide the molar mass of the compound by the mass of the empirical formula.
  3. Multiply the subscripts in the empirical formula by the answer from step 2.
