Part1

Steps to Solving Heat Problems

A Regents heat question can involve any of the three heat equations. Choosing the wrong one is the most common mistake, so the first step is always to decide what is happening to the substance. In this section, we will practice the five steps with all three equations.

Steps to Solving Heat Problems

The same five steps work for every heat problem. Once the equation is chosen, listing the variables with their units makes it clear which value goes where.

  • Steps to solving heat problems:
    1. Identify which equation to use. If the temperature changes, use q = mcΔT. If there is a solid ↔ liquid change, use q = mHf. If there is a liquid ↔ gas change, use q = mHv.
    2. List the variables and the unknown, each with its unit.
    3. Substitute the variables and their units into the equation.
    4. Solve for the unknown algebraically.
    5. Write the final answer with units.
  • Read the problem for clues to the equation:
    • A starting and an ending temperature → a temperature change → q = mcΔT
    • Melts, freezes or fusion, at the melting point → q = mHf
    • Boils, vaporizes, evaporates or condenses, at the boiling point → q = mHv

Combining the Heat Equations

Turning an ice cube from the freezer into a glass of cool water takes three steps: warming the ice to 0 °C, melting it, and warming the liquid water. Each step is one segment of the heating curve and needs its own equation. In this section, we will add up the heat for several segments.

Heat for Several Segments

A heating curve shows every segment a sample passes through. To find the total heat, find the heat for each segment separately, then add them together.

  • Each sloped segment uses q = mcΔT with the specific heat capacity of that phase (ice, liquid water and steam each have their own value). Each flat segment uses q = mHf or q = mHv.
  • The total heat is the sum of the heat for every segment: qtotal = q1 + q2 + q3 + …
