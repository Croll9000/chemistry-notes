Balancing Chemical Reactions

A recipe that calls for 2 cups of flour still needs 2 cups of flour at the end of the bowl: the ingredients only get rearranged. A chemical equation works the same way. Every atom that goes into a reaction comes out of it, so the equation has to show the same atoms on both sides.

  • Even though compounds are broken down and formed during chemical reactions, atoms are neither created nor destroyed.
  • The total number of atoms of each element must remain the same before and after a reaction takes place.
  • Often reactions must be balanced by adding coefficients in front of chemical formulas to ensure that there are the same number of atoms on each side of the reaction.

Steps to Balancing Chemical Reactions

  1. Count the number of atoms of each element on both sides of the reaction.
  2. Add whole-number coefficients (never subscripts) in front of formulas to balance the number of atoms on the reactants and products side.
  3. Put the ratio of coefficients for the balanced reaction in lowest terms.
  • Tips:
    • Start with elements that are found only once on each side of the equation.
    • Treat polyatomic ions as a single unit if they do not break up.
    • When you have an even number of atoms on one side of the equation and an odd number on the other, it is often useful to multiply the odd number by 2, even if it doesn’t immediately balance the number of atoms.
