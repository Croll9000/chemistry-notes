Multistep Conversions

Mass, volume and particles can all be converted into each other by going through moles. With the mole ratio, a mass of one substance can be turned into a mass of another.

The Mole Triangle

Mole Triangle

A 1.00-kg bag of table salt contains 17.1 moles, or 1.03 × 1025 formula units, of sodium chloride. Mass, number of particles and, for gases, volume are three different ways to describe how much of a substance there is, but none of them can be converted directly into another. Moles are the hub that connects all three, so every conversion in this unit passes through moles. The mole triangle is a visual aid for converting between these quantities, with moles in the middle and the calculation for each conversion written on its arrow. The steps for using the mole triangle are:

  1. Start at the given unit on the mole triangle.
  2. Move in the direction of the desired unit.
  3. Follow the calculation shown on each arrow.

Mass–Mass Conversions

Mass–Mass Conversions

Burning 1.00 kg of charcoal, which is mostly carbon, produces 3.66 kg of carbon dioxide. The mass increases because each carbon atom combines with two oxygen atoms from the air. If we know the mass of one substance consumed or produced in a reaction, we can determine the mass of another substance in the same reaction, but their masses are not necessarily equal. Substances react in the mole ratio given by the balanced equation, not in a mass ratio, so the route from one mass to another always goes through moles. The steps for performing mass–mass conversions are:

  1. Convert the mass of the given substance into moles using its molar mass.
  2. Convert the moles of the given substance into moles of the target substance using the mole ratio.
  3. Convert the moles of the target substance into mass using its molar mass.
  • If we are given the mass of a substance consumed or produced in a reaction, we can determine the corresponding mass of another substance consumed or produced in the same reaction.
    • However, their masses are not necessarily equal.
    • Substances must react with one another in the mole ratio given by the balanced equation.
  • Steps to Performing Mass–Mass Conversions:

    2. Convert the moles of the given substance into moles of the target substance using the mole ratio.
    3. Convert the moles of the target substance into mass using its molar mass.

Multistep Mole Conversions

Multistep Mole Conversions

When heated, a teaspoon (4.6 g) of baking soda releases about 0.6 L of carbon dioxide at STP, which helps quick breads rise. Finding that volume connects three different units: grams of baking soda, moles of each substance and liters of gas. Real problems often mix units like this, so we join two mole triangles with the mole ratio, which covers every possible combination. The steps for performing multistep mole conversions are:

  1. Convert the given unit into moles.
  2. Convert moles of the given substance into moles of the target substance (if necessary).
  3. Convert moles of the target substance into the desired unit.
  • Steps to Performing Multistep Mole Conversions:
    1. Convert the given unit into moles.
    2. Convert moles of the given substance into moles of the target substance (if necessary).
    3. Convert moles of the target substance into the desired unit.

Three conversion factors: into moles, mole ratio, out of moles.
