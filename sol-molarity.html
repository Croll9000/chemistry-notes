Molarity

The saline solution in a hospital IV bag contains 9.0 g of sodium chloride in every liter, a concentration of 0.154 mol/L that matches the salt content of blood. A solution much weaker or much stronger would damage blood cells, so the concentration has to be measured exactly. Chemists measure the concentration of most laboratory solutions in moles of solute per liter of solution. In this section we will discuss molarity and how to calculate it.

Molarity

Molarity counts the solute in moles, so solutions with the same molarity contain the same number of solute particles in the same volume, no matter which solute they contain.

  • Molarity (M) – the number of moles of solute per liter of solution.
  • The unit of molarity is mol/L, written M and read “molar.” A 2.0 M solution contains 2.0 moles of solute in every liter of solution.
  • The volume is the volume of the whole solution, not of the water alone. To make a solution, the solute is dissolved first and then water is added up to the mark on a volumetric flask.
n=MV
n – moles of solute (mol)M – molarity (M, or mol/L)V – volume of solution (L)
  • Steps to solving molarity problems:
    1. List the variables and the unknown, each with its unit.
    2. Convert units if needed: grams to moles with the molar mass, milliliters to liters.
    3. Write down the equation.
    4. Substitute and solve. Substitute the values and their units, then solve for the unknown algebraically.
    5. Write the final answer with units.

Part2

Dilution

Hospitals and laboratories buy hydrochloric acid at about 12 M and dilute it with water to the strength they need. Adding water does not change how many moles of acid are in the container; it only spreads them through a larger volume. In this section we will discuss how to calculate the concentration or volume of a diluted solution.

Dilution

When a solution is diluted, the moles of solute stay the same, so the product of molarity and volume before dilution equals the product after dilution.

  • Dilution – adding solvent to a solution to lower its concentration.
  • The moles of solute do not change: M1V1 = M2V2
  • The volume of water to add is the final volume minus the starting volume.
M1V1=M2V2
M1, V1 – molarity and volume before dilutionM2, V2 – molarity and volume after dilution
