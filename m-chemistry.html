Chemistry and Matter

Chemistry is the study of matter.

Chemistry

Science is a field which seeks to describe and explain the world around us. Most of what we see in our world is matter, and Chemistry is the branch of science tasked with studying matter, its composition and the ways it reacts.

  • Chemistry – the study of matter: its properties, composition and how it reacts.

What Is Matter?

Everything that can be touched, held or weighed is made of matter, from the desk in front of you to the air you breathe to your own body. Since chemistry is the study of matter, it is important to define exactly what qualifies as matter.

  • Matter – anything that has mass and takes up volume (space).
  • Mass – how much “stuff” is in an object.
    • Unit – grams (g) or kilograms (kg)
    • Measuring device – balance
  • Volume – how much space an object takes up.
    • Unit – milliliters (mL), liters (L) or cubic centimeters (cm³)
    • Measuring device – graduated cylinder (liquids); ruler (regular solids)
Your pencil would be an example of matter.
Most things that you can see and touch every day would be considered matter.

Air is also matter, even though it is invisible. A balloon full of air has mass and takes up space. Mass and volume are measured on the Density & Identifying Substances page.

What Isn’t Matter?

Light, heat and electricity are real, but they have no mass and take up no space. They are forms of energy.

What Isn’t Matter?

Not everything in the universe would be classified as matter. Energy, for example, does not have mass nor does it take up space. However, it is very real, and can be experienced in the form of light, heat and electricity.

Heat, light and electricity are forms of energy and therefore not classified as matter.

Matter or Not?

Energy

  • Energy – the ability to do work or cause change.
    • Unit – joule (J), kilojoule (kJ)
      • 1 kJ = 1000 J
    • It represents the ability to create a change in a system.
  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed, but it can be converted from one form into another.
Form of energyDescriptionExample
ChemicalStored in the bonds of substancesGasoline, food, a battery
ElectricalCarried by moving chargesCurrent in a wire
LightEnergy carried by lightSunlight, a light bulb
Heat (thermal)Energy that flows from a hotter object to a colder oneA hot stove, a flame
KineticEnergy of motionA moving car
Energy can take on different forms such as when chemical energy in a battery transforms into electrical energy as it passes through a wire and then into light energy in a lightbulb.
An automobile will stay motionless until you fill it with gasoline. Gasoline contains chemical energy. The energy causes a change, allowing the car to move.
