Lewis Dot Diagrams

When it comes to participating in chemical reactions, the most important electrons are those in the outermost shell, referred to as valence electrons. These are the electrons that participate in chemical bonding. Therefore, chemists often use a shorthand visual representation of the outermost electrons known as a Lewis Dot Diagram.

  • Lewis Dot Diagram – the element’s symbol surrounded by dots that represent its valence electrons.
    • Each dot represents one valence electron.
  • The symbol stands for the nucleus and all of the inner (core) electrons.

To draw a Lewis dot diagram:

  1. Write the element symbol.
  2. Find the number of valence electrons: the last number in the shell notation, or from the group (groups 1–2: the group number; groups 13–18: the group number minus 10).
  3. Place the dots one at a time on the four sides of the symbol (top, right, bottom, left) before pairing any of them.
    • There can be at most 2 dots on a side (8 total).
Phosphorus is 2-8-5, so it has 5 valence electrons. One dot goes on each side, then the 5th dot pairs up with one of them.

Helium is the exception: its 2 valence electrons are drawn as a pair, because its only energy level is full.

Lewis Dot Diagrams of Ions

Ions, atoms that have gained or lost electrons, are represented slightly differently than atoms in Lewis Dot Diagrams.

  • Rules for drawing an ion:
    • Surround the symbol and its dots with square brackets [ ].
    • Write the charge in the top right corner, outside the brackets.
  • A cation has lost its valence electrons, so it is drawn with no dots inside its brackets.
  • An anion has gained electrons until it has 8 dots inside its brackets.
