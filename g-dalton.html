Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressures

Dry air at sea level has a total pressure of 101.3 kPa. Of this, nitrogen supplies about 79.1 kPa, oxygen about 21.2 kPa and argon about 0.9 kPa. Gas particles are so far apart that a particle of one gas behaves as if the other gases were not there: each gas in a mixture collides with the walls of the container on its own, so each one contributes its own share of the pressure. The pressure that one gas in a mixture would exert if it occupied the same container by itself, at the same temperature, is referred to as its partial pressure. Since pressure comes from collisions with the walls, the more particles of a gas there are, the greater its partial pressure.

In 1801, John Dalton noticed that the pressure of moist air equals the pressure of the dry air plus the pressure of the water vapor in it. This relationship is known as Dalton’s law of partial pressures: the total pressure of a mixture of gases equals the sum of the partial pressures of the gases in it. The steps for solving Dalton’s law problems are:

  1. List the variables and the unknown. Write each partial pressure, with its unit.
  2. Write down Dalton’s law.
  3. Substitute and solve. Add the partial pressures, or subtract them to find a missing one.
  4. Write the final answer with units.
  • In a mixture of different gases, each gas applies a fraction of the total pressure of the mixture. This fraction is the partial pressure of the gas.
    • The pressure applied by a gas is due to the collisions its particles have with the walls of the container, so the greater the number of particles of a gas, the greater the pressure that gas applies.
    • Partial Pressure – the pressure applied by a single component gas within a mixture if it occupied the same container by itself at the same temperature.
  • Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressures – the total pressure of a mixture of gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of those component gases.

Mole Fractions

A scuba tank of nitrox holds 32 moles of oxygen for every 68 moles of nitrogen. If the tank is filled to 200 atm, the oxygen supplies 32% of that pressure, about 64 atm, because oxygen makes up 32% of the particles in the tank. Every particle in a gas mixture contributes to the pressure in the same way, so the pressure of each gas is directly proportional to the number of its particles. A gas’s share of the total pressure therefore matches its share of the total moles.

The ratio of the moles of one gas to the total moles of gas in the mixture is referred to as the mole fraction of that gas. The partial pressure of a gas equals its mole fraction multiplied by the total pressure. The steps for solving mole fraction problems are:
