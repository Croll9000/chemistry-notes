Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressures

Dry air at sea level has a total pressure of 101.3 kPa, of which nitrogen supplies about 79.1 kPa, oxygen about 21.2 kPa and argon about 0.9 kPa. Each gas in a mixture collides with the walls independently of the others, so each contributes its own share of the pressure. In this section, we will discuss Dalton’s law of partial pressures.

Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressures

In 1801, John Dalton noticed that the pressure of moist air equals the pressure of the dry air plus the pressure of the water vapor in it. Gas particles are so far apart that a particle of one gas behaves as if the other gases were not there.

  • In a mixture of different gases, each gas applies a fraction of the total pressure of the mixture. This fraction is the partial pressure of the gas.
    • The pressure applied by a gas is due to the collisions its particles have with the walls of the container, so the greater the number of particles of a gas, the greater the pressure that gas applies.
    • Partial Pressure – the pressure applied by a single component gas within a mixture if it occupied the same container by itself at the same temperature.
  • Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressures – the total pressure of a mixture of gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of those component gases.
  • Steps to solving Dalton’s law problems:
    1. List the variables and the unknown. Write each partial pressure, with its unit.
    2. Write down Dalton’s law.
    3. Substitute and solve. Add the partial pressures, or subtract them to find a missing one.
    4. Write the final answer with units.
Ptotal=P1 + P2 + P3 + …
Ptotal – total pressure applied by the mixture of gases (atm or kPa)P1 – partial pressure of gas 1 (atm or kPa)P2 – partial pressure of gas 2 (atm or kPa)

Mole Fractions

A scuba tank of nitrox holds 32 moles of oxygen for every 68 moles of nitrogen. If the tank is at 200 atm, the oxygen supplies 32% of that pressure, about 64 atm, because the oxygen makes up 32% of the particles. In this section, we will use mole fractions to find the partial pressure of each gas in a mixture.

Mole Fractions

Since every particle in a gas mixture contributes to the pressure in the same way, a gas’s share of the pressure matches its share of the particles.

  • The pressure applied by a gas is directly proportional to the number of gas particles. This means that in a mixture of gases, the greater the number of particles there are for a specific gas, the larger the partial pressure it will exert.
  • The partial pressure of a specific gas in a mixture is proportional to the ratio of moles of that gas to the total number of moles of gas in the mixture. This ratio is called the mole fraction of the gas.
  • Steps to solving mole fraction problems:
    1. List the variables and the unknown. Find the total moles, nT, by adding the moles of every gas.
    2. Write down the equation.
    3. Substitute and solve.
    4. Write the final answer with units.
P1PT=n1nTorP1=n1nT(PT)
P1 – partial pressure of gas 1 (atm or kPa)PT – total pressure applied by the mixture of gases (atm or kPa)n1 – moles of gas 1 (mol)nT – total moles of gas in the mixture (mol)
