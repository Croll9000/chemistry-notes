Percent Composition

Iron makes up 69.9% of the mass of hematite, Fe₂O₃, the iron ore mined most often. This percent never changes, because every formula unit of hematite contains exactly 2 iron atoms and 3 oxygen atoms. In this section, we will calculate the percent by mass of each element in a compound from its formula.

Percent Composition

Mining companies need to know how much metal they can extract from a ton of ore, and nutrition labels list how much of a food is protein or fat. Both are percent composition problems. Because every compound has a fixed formula, each element always makes up the same percent of its mass.

  • Percent Composition – the percent by mass of each element in a compound.
  • % composition = (mass of part ÷ mass of whole) × 100
    • Mass of part: the mass of one element in one mole of the compound (number of atoms × atomic mass).
    • Mass of whole: the molar mass of the compound.
Example
Water, H2O (18.02 g/mol): % H = (2.02 ÷ 18.02) × 100 = 11.2%; % O = (16.00 ÷ 18.02) × 100 = 88.8%

Hydrated Compounds

Gypsum, the mineral used to make drywall, is calcium sulfate dihydrate, CaSO₄•2H₂O, and is about 21% water by mass. The water is not wet: its molecules are locked into the crystal in a fixed ratio of 2 water molecules for every formula unit of calcium sulfate. In this section, we will name hydrates and calculate the percent of water they contain.

Hydrated Compounds

Bright blue copper(II) sulfate crystals turn white when they are heated, and the white powder turns blue again when a drop of water is added. The color change comes from water molecules that are built into the crystal itself. Desiccant packets in shoe boxes work the same way: their crystals soak up water from the air.

  • Ionic compounds exist as crystal lattices, geometric arrangements of alternating cations and anions.
    • Sometimes molecules of water become embedded in the crystal lattice structure.
    • These are known as hydrated compounds.
    • Ionic lattices without water are known as anhydrous compounds.
    • Hydrated Compound (Hydrate) – compound (usually ionic) that contains water.
    • Anhydrous Compound – compound that does not contain water.
  • Copper(II) chloride can become hydrated by absorbing water molecules into its lattice structure.

Naming Hydrated Compounds

  • When water becomes part of the crystal lattice of a hydrate, it does so in a fixed ratio. When naming hydrated compounds it is necessary to specify the ratio of ionic formula units to water molecules.
  • Steps for Naming Hydrated Compounds:
    1. Name the ionic compound.
    2. Include a prefix to show the number of water molecules for every formula unit of the ionic compound.
    3. Attach the ending “-hydrate” after the prefix.
  • Steps for Writing the Formula of a Hydrate:
    1. Write the formula for the ionic compound.
    2. Identify the number of water molecules per formula unit from the prefix, and write it after a dot: •nH2O.

Percent of Water in a Hydrate

  • Heating a hydrate drives the water out of the crystal as water vapor. The anhydrous compound is left behind.
  • The percent of water by mass can be found from a lab measurement or from the formula:
    • From the lab: % water = (mass of water lost ÷ mass of hydrate) × 100
    • From the formula: % water = (mass of water in one mole of the hydrate ÷ molar mass of the hydrate) × 100
