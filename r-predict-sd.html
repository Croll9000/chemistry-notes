Predicting Synthesis Reactions

Two elements combine into one compound.

Predicting Synthesis Reactions

Sodium, a soft metal, and chlorine, a poisonous gas, combine to form table salt. When two elements react, the product can be predicted from the types of elements involved.

  • In a synthesis reaction, two or more simple substances combine to form one more complex product.
  • Steps to Predicting Synthesis Reactions:
    1. Identify the types of reactants: a metal and a nonmetal, or two nonmetals (such as hydrogen and a nonmetal).
    2. Write the formula for the product.
      • Metal + nonmetal → ionic compound: criss-cross the charges of the ions and reduce if necessary.
      • Hydrogen + nonmetal → molecular compound: combine the atoms in the ratio that fills each element’s valence shell (count the bonds each atom forms).
    3. Balance the equation. Remember the diatomic elements (H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2, I2).
Part2

Predicting Decomposition Reactions

A binary compound breaks into its elements.

Predicting Decomposition Reactions

Passing electricity through water splits it into hydrogen gas and oxygen gas. A compound made of two elements breaks down into those same two elements.

  • In a decomposition reaction, one compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances. Energy (heat or electricity) is often needed.
  • Binary compound – a compound made of only two elements. Ex: NaCl, H2O, Al2O3
  • A binary compound decomposes into its two elements.
  • Steps to Predicting Decomposition Reactions:
    1. Identify the two elements in the binary compound.
    2. Write each element as a product in its standard form. Diatomic elements are written as H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2 and I2.
    3. Balance the equation.
  • Decomposition of a binary compound is the reverse of the synthesis of that compound.

Oxides in Water

Metal oxides make bases; nonmetal oxides make acids.

Oxides in Water and Acid Rain

Statues and buildings made of limestone and marble slowly wear away in polluted cities. Gases released by burning fossil fuels dissolve in rainwater and form acids.

  • Oxide – a compound of an element with oxygen.
  • When metal oxides combine with water, they produce basic solutions of metal hydroxides. Ex: CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2
  • When nonmetal oxides combine with water, they produce acids. Ex: CO2 + H2O → H2CO3 (carbonic acid)
  • This process can lead to the formation of acid rain.
    • Burning fossil fuels releases nonmetal oxides such as sulfur dioxide (SO2), nitrogen dioxide (NO2) and carbon dioxide (CO2) into the atmosphere.
    • These nonmetal oxides combine with water (and oxygen) in the atmosphere to produce acids such as sulfuric acid (H2SO4), nitric acid (HNO3) and carbonic acid (H2CO3).
    • Acid rain lowers the pH of lakes and soil and damages forests and buildings.

More Synthesis and Decomposition Patterns

Compounds that combine or break apart in predictable ways.

Synthesis with Compounds

Synthesis is not limited to elements. Compounds such as metal oxides and water also combine in predictable ways.

  • Synthesis reactions that involve compounds depend on the specific types of substances combining.
  • Steps to Predicting Synthesis Reactions (Compounds):
    1. Identify the types of substances combining.
    2. Use the table to find the product. If the product is ionic, criss-cross the charges of the ions to determine its formula and simplify if necessary.
    3. Balance the equation.

Decomposition of Compounds with Polyatomic Ions

Heating limestone (calcium carbonate) produces lime and carbon dioxide, a reaction used to make cement. Compounds that contain polyatomic ions break down in patterns that depend on the ion.

  • Decomposition reactions of compounds that are not binary depend on the specific class of compound breaking down.
  • Steps to Predicting Decomposition Reactions (Compounds):
    1. Identify the type of compound breaking down.
    2. Use the table to find the products. If a product is ionic, criss-cross the charges of the ions to determine its formula and simplify if necessary.
    3. Balance the equation.
