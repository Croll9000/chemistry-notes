Spontaneous and Nonspontaneous Changes

Spontaneous and Nonspontaneous

Despite the advertising slogan, diamonds are not forever. Diamond slowly turns into graphite, the gray carbon in pencil lead, because graphite is the more stable form of carbon. The change is so slow, however, that a diamond ring will look the same after a billion years. In chemistry, a change that will happen on its own is called spontaneous, even when, like this one, it is far too slow to notice.

A spontaneous change is a reaction or change that will occur on its own under the given conditions, without a continuous supply of energy from outside. A nonspontaneous change is a reaction or change that will not occur under the given conditions unless energy is continuously supplied. Spontaneous does not mean fast. Rusting and diamond turning into graphite are spontaneous but slow, since how fast a reaction goes is a question of kinetics, not thermodynamics. Additionally, if a change is spontaneous, the reverse change is nonspontaneous under the same conditions: ice melts spontaneously at 25 °C, while water does not freeze at 25 °C.

Two driving forces make a change spontaneous: a decrease in energy (exothermic, ΔH < 0) and an increase in entropy (ΔS > 0). When both forces favor a change, it is always spontaneous, and when both oppose it, it never is. When they pull in opposite directions, whether a change is spontaneous depends on the temperature.

  • Nonspontaneous – a reaction or change that will not occur under the given conditions unless energy is continuously supplied.
  • Spontaneous does not mean fast. Rusting and diamond turning into graphite are spontaneous but slow; how fast a reaction goes is kinetics, not thermodynamics.
  • If a change is spontaneous, the reverse change is nonspontaneous under the same conditions. Ice melts spontaneously at 25 °C; water does not freeze at 25 °C.
