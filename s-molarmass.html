Molar Mass

The mass of one mole of a substance.

Significance of a Mole

Nobody can count out 6.02 × 1023 atoms, but anyone can weigh a sample on a balance. The number 6.02 × 1023 was chosen so that the mass of one mole of any substance, in grams, matches its atomic mass on the periodic table. That choice turns every balance in a lab into a particle counter.

  • The unit of a mole has significance because it represents the number of atoms in 12 g of C-12. Recall that C-12 has a mass of 12 u. This means that if you possess 1 mole of a substance, the mass, in grams (g), of that substance is equal to its atomic mass in unified atomic mass units (u).
  • The mass of 1 mole of any atom or molecule, in grams, is equal to its atomic mass (or the total mass of its atoms).
Each sample contains the same number of particles, 6.02 × 1023, but the masses are different because the particles have different masses.

Molar Mass

When carrying out reactions, chemists must measure out the right number of moles of each substance. To do that with a balance, they need to know the mass of one mole of each substance.

  • Molar Mass – mass of one mole of a substance.
    • Unit – grams/mole (g/mol)
  • Calculating molar mass:
    • Elements: look up the atomic mass of the element on the periodic table. The value of the atomic mass in “u” is equal to the mass of 1 mole in “g.” Ex: 1 mole of Mg = 24.31 g
    • Compounds: add up the masses from the periodic table of the individual atoms that make up the compound.

Mole–Mass Conversions

A pharmacist preparing a medicine and a chemist running a reaction both work in moles, but their balances read in grams. Converting between moles and mass is the most common calculation in a chemistry lab.

  • It is often necessary to convert from moles to mass or from mass to moles. To do so we use the molar mass of the substance.
    • To convert from number of moles to mass, multiply by the molar mass of the substance.
    • To convert from mass to number of moles, divide by the molar mass of the substance.
  • The molar mass of a substance can be used as a conversion factor.
