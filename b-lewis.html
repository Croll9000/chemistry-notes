Drawing Lewis Structures (Covalent)

Carbon dioxide makes up about 0.04% of the air, and each of its molecules contains a carbon atom joined to two oxygen atoms by double bonds. A Lewis structure shows exactly how the atoms in a molecule share their valence electrons so that every atom ends with a full valence shell. Consider a water molecule, which contains 8 valence electrons: 6 from the oxygen atom and 1 from each hydrogen atom. To draw it, we start with the Lewis dot diagram of each atom, place the atom that can form the most bonds in the center, and circle pairs of electrons between the central atom and the outer atoms to show covalent bonds. Bonded electrons count toward both atoms in the bond, and hydrogen is stable with only 2. Steps for drawing Lewis structures for covalent molecules:

  1. Draw the Lewis dot diagram for each atom in the compound.
  2. Determine which atom can form the most bonds and place that in the center of the molecule.
  3. Circle pairs of electrons between the central atom and outer atoms to represent covalent bonds.
  4. After each bond is formed, stop and count the number of valence electrons around each atom.
    • Remember that bonded (circled) electrons count towards both atoms in the bond.
  5. If necessary, form double or triple bonds.
  6. Stop when each atom has a full valence shell.
    • Hydrogen is stable with 2 valence electrons.
  7. Redraw any circled electrons as lines to represent covalent bonds.
  • Lone Pair – a pair of valence electrons that is not shared with another atom.

Draw Lewis structures step by step, the same way as the ionic drawer: draw each atom’s dots, drag the atoms into place, circle shared pairs, then redraw the molecule with lines and lone pairs.
