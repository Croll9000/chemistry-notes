Percent by Mass and Percent by Volume

Percent Concentration

A bottle of rubbing alcohol from the drugstore is labeled “70% isopropyl alcohol.” This means that every 100 mL of the liquid contains 70 mL of isopropyl alcohol, with water making up the rest. Percents are the concentration units printed on most household products, because they can be measured with nothing more than a balance or a graduated cylinder, and they need no moles at all.

Percent by mass is the mass of solute in every 100 g of solution, and it is usually used for solids dissolved in liquids, such as the sugar in a soft drink. Percent by volume is the volume of solute in every 100 mL of solution, and it is usually used for liquids mixed with liquids, such as the alcohol in rubbing alcohol, since liquids are easier to measure by volume than by mass.

In both equations, the amount of the whole solution is in the denominator, not the amount of solvent. A solution made from 15 g of salt and 85 g of water is 15% salt, because the 15 g is compared with the 100 g of solution, not the 85 g of water. For percent by volume, the volume of the solution is measured after mixing. This matters because liquid volumes do not always add: mixing 70 mL of alcohol with 30 mL of water gives slightly less than 100 mL of solution, as the smaller water molecules fit into the spaces between the alcohol molecules.

To solve a percent concentration problem:

  1. List the variables and the unknown, each with its unit.
  2. Find the amount of the solution, if needed, by adding the solute and the solvent.
  3. Write down the equation.
  4. Substitute and solve.
  5. Write the final answer with a percent sign.
