Properties of Matter

Properties describe matter. Some can be observed without changing the substance; others only show up when it reacts.

Physical and Chemical Properties

A physical property can be observed without producing a new substance.

Describing Matter

Gold is used in jewelry and electronics because it is shiny, does not tarnish and conducts electricity well. Copper is used in electrical wiring for similar reasons. The way a material is used depends on its characteristics, which is why chemists study them closely.

  • Property – characteristic that describes matter.
    • Understanding the properties of a substance can allow us to identify it and predict the ways in which it will react.

Types of Properties

  • Physical Property – measurable characteristic of a substance that can be determined without changing the identity of that substance
    • Ex: temperature, mass, volume, density, melting point, boiling point
  • Chemical Property – characteristic of a substance that describes the way it reacts.
    • Can only be determined by carrying out a chemical reaction, which changes the identity of that substance.
    • Ex: flammable, corrosive, highly reactive, inert

Intensive and Extensive Properties

An extensive property depends on the amount of substance. An intensive property does not.

Intensive vs. Extensive Properties

Some characteristics of a sample depend on how much of it there is, while others do not. A single drop of water and an entire swimming pool have very different masses, yet both boil at the same temperature.

  • Physical properties can be categorized as either intensive or extensive.
    • Intensive Properties – properties which do not change with the size of the sample.
      • Intensive properties are important because they are the same for every sample of a substance, so they can be used to identify a substance.
      • Ex: density, melting point, boiling point, color
    • Extensive Properties – properties that change with the size of the sample.
      • Extensive properties can’t identify a substance: a large piece of one metal and a small piece of another could have the same mass.
      • Ex: mass, volume, length
Consider two samples of the same material, copper. One block is small while the other block is large. Despite their different sizes, they have the same density, melting point and boiling point. Therefore, these are intensive properties. On the other hand, their masses and volumes differ. These are extensive properties.

Physical and chemical properties match the two kinds of change: physical changes and chemical changes.
