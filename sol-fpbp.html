Calculating Freezing and Boiling Points

ΔT = iKm

Many cooks add salt to a pot of water “to make it boil hotter.” If we calculate the effect, it turns out to be almost nothing. A tablespoon of salt, about 17 g or 0.29 mol, dissolved in 4 kg of water gives a molality of about 0.073 m. Each formula unit forms two ions, and each mole of dissolved particles in a kilogram of water raises the boiling point by 0.512 °C, so the boiling point rises by only 2 × 0.512 °C/m × 0.073 m ≈ 0.07 °C. The salt makes the pasta taste better, but it does not make the water noticeably hotter.

That calculation used the equations for freezing point depression and boiling point elevation. For a dilute solution, the change in freezing or boiling point is directly proportional to the molality of the dissolved particles. The molality of the particles is the molality of the solute times the number of particles each formula unit or molecule forms, a number referred to as the van ’t Hoff factor (i): 1 for sugar, 2 for NaCl and 3 for CaCl2. The constant K tells how many degrees the temperature changes for each mole of particles in a kilogram of solvent, and it is different for every solvent:

For water, the constants are Kf = 1.86 °C/m and Kb = 0.512 °C/m, so dissolved particles lower the freezing point of water more than three times as much as they raise its boiling point. The equations give only the size of the change, ΔT, so the last step is always to apply it in the correct direction: the freezing point of the solution is the normal freezing point minus ΔT, while the boiling point is the normal boiling point plus ΔT. To calculate a new freezing or boiling point:

  1. List the variables and the unknown, each with its unit.
  2. Find i and the molality (convert grams to moles and grams of solvent to kilograms).
  3. Write down the equation.
  4. Substitute and solve for ΔT.
  5. Subtract ΔT from the freezing point, or add it to the boiling point, and write the final answer with units.
  • The van ’t Hoff factor (i) – the number of particles one formula unit or molecule forms when it dissolves (1 for sugar, 2 for NaCl, 3 for CaCl2).
  • For water, Kf = 1.86 °C/m and Kb = 0.512 °C/m.
  • The freezing point of the solution is the normal freezing point minus ΔT; the boiling point is the normal boiling point plus ΔT.
