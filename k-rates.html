Average Rate

Average Rate

A car that travels 120 miles in 2 hours has an average speed of 60 miles per hour, even though its speedometer showed many different speeds along the way. The average describes the whole trip, without saying what happened at any one moment. A reaction rate can be described the same way, by the total change in concentration over a period of time.

The average rate is the change in the concentration of a reactant or product over a period of time. Since reactants are used up, their concentration decreases, so a minus sign is placed in front of each reactant’s change to keep the rate positive. Consider the reaction N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3. For every 1 mole of N2 used up, 3 moles of H2 are used up and 2 moles of NH3 form, so hydrogen disappears three times as fast as nitrogen. To give one rate for the whole reaction, each change is divided by the coefficient of that substance in the balanced equation, so that the rate is the same no matter which substance is measured.

To solve an average rate problem:

  • Average rate – the change in the concentration of a reactant or product over a period of time.
    • rate=−Δ[reactant]Δt=Δ[product]Δt
    [ ] – concentration (M)Δt – change in time (s)
  • Reactants are used up, so their concentration decreases; the minus sign keeps the rate positive.
  • Each change is divided by the coefficient of that substance in the balanced equation, so that the rate is the same no matter which substance is measured. For aA + bB → cC + dD:
rate=−1aΔ[A]Δt=−1bΔ[B]Δt=1cΔ[C]Δt=1dΔ[D]Δt
  • Steps to solving average rate problems:
    1. Write the balanced equation.
    2. Write the rate expression for each substance, dividing by its coefficient.
    3. Substitute the change you know and solve for the unknown change.
    4. Write the final answer with units.

Instantaneous Rate

Instantaneous Rate

A speedometer shows how fast a car is going at one moment, not its average for the whole trip. Reactions usually start fast and slow down as the reactants are used up, so the rate at one moment can be very different from the average rate over a longer time. A chemist who wants to know how fast a reaction is going right now needs the instantaneous rate.

The instantaneous rate is the change in the concentration of a reactant or product at a single moment in time. On a graph of concentration against time, the two kinds of rate are both slopes. The average rate is the slope of a secant line drawn between two points on the curve, while the instantaneous rate is the slope of a tangent line drawn at one point on the curve, a line that just touches the curve at that point.

The steepest part of the curve is almost always the beginning. Most reactions are fastest at the start, when the reactant concentration is highest, and slow down as the reactants are used up, since fewer reactant particles mean fewer collisions per second. Thus, the curve flattens out over time until the reaction stops.

  • Instantaneous rate – the change in the concentration of a reactant or product at a single moment in time.
  • The average rate is the slope of a secant line drawn between two points on the curve.
  • The instantaneous rate is the slope of a tangent line drawn at one point on the curve.
  • Most reactions are fastest at the start, when the reactant concentration is highest, and slow down as the reactants are used up.
